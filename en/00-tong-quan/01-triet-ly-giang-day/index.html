Teaching philosophy

The principles that guide how guitar is taught and learned at Agora Academy.

Programme goals

  • Students play real music — songs and pieces — not just technical exercises.
  • Correct technique from the start, so there are no "bad habits" to undo later.
  • Students can practise on their own, listen to themselves and self-correct.
  • Students keep enjoying the guitar for the long term, even after the course ends.

Teaching principles

  • Foundations and songs together: every new technique is tied to a specific piece of music.
  • Technique serves the music: always ask "does playing it this way sound better?".
  • Relaxation first, speed later: comfortable posture, no tension; slow and correct beats fast and wrong.
  • One fix at a time: correct only the single most important thing each time.
  • Students play more than the teacher talks: aim for students playing ≥ 50% of the lesson.
  • Little and often: 15–20 minutes a day beats 2 hours at the weekend.
  • Listen a lot: listen to a reference recording before practising; listen back to yourself on video.
  • Respect each student's pace: never compare students with each other; compare them with themselves a month ago.

Classical and đệm hát: two tracks, one foundation

  • Both tracks share the same foundation: posture, rhythm, chords, hand independence.
  • Classical students still learn basic đệm hát (accompanying singing) — so they can play with friends and family.
  • Accompaniment students still learn right-hand finger technique (p-i-m-a) — for fingerstyle and arpeggios (rải hợp âm).
  • Respect every genre: Trịnh Công Sơn songs, bolero and pop are all music worth studying seriously.

Online learning: use its strengths, accept its limits

  • Strength: learning from home with no travel time; every lesson and homework can be recorded and rewatched.
  • Strength: homework videos make students play a piece all the way through and listen back to themselves.
  • Limit: network latency means teacher and student cannot play at the same time.
  • Limit: the teacher cannot physically adjust the student's hands → describe clearly, demonstrate, use close-up camera angles.
  • How to handle this: see Lesson structure.

The teacher's role

  • Model: play for the student first, play slowly, break it down.
  • Observer: watch the left hand, right hand and posture closely on camera.
  • Encourager: acknowledge small progress, help students through hard phases (sore fingertips, barre chords).
  • Guide: help students choose a track and pieces that suit their taste.

The student's role

  • Practise regularly as assigned and submit videos on time.
  • Speak up when something is unclear or hurts — there is no such thing as a "silly" question.
  • Have the guitar tuned and the camera and audio ready before the lesson.

What we avoid

  • Teaching students to "memorise tabs" without understanding rhythm and chords.
  • Cramming too much new material into one lesson.
  • Criticising, comparing, or correcting too many mistakes at once.
  • Using material that infringes copyright (song lyrics, copied tabs).
