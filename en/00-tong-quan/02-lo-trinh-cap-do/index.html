Level pathway

Draft

4 levels, duration, entry / exit requirements and how the Classical and Accompaniment tracks split.

Overview

  • 4 levels, each ~12 sessions × 60 minutes.
  • At 1 session a week → each level takes about 3 months; at 2 sessions a week → about 6–7 weeks.
  • A "lesson" in these materials is not always one session: a hard lesson may take 2 sessions, easy ones may be combined.
  • Each level ends with a review and assessment session; students move up only when they meet the requirements.

Level 1 — Beginner (Nhập môn)

  • Entry: has never played, or is self-taught but posture and rhythm are not yet solid.
  • Content: getting to know the guitar, posture, tuning, basic right / left hand, reading tab and chord diagrams, the chords Em, Am, C, G, D, rhythm and the metronome.
  • Exit:
    • Sits with correct posture, tunes with an app.
    • Changes between the basic chords within 4 beats at ~60 bpm.
    • Keeps steady time with a metronome.
    • Plays a simple accompaniment pattern and a simple melody (a taster of both tracks).

Level 2 — Foundation (Cơ bản)

  • Entry: meets the Level 1 exit requirements.
  • Core: p-i-m-a arpeggios, 7th and sus chords, barre chords F and Bm.
  • Classical track exit: reads treble-clef notation on all 6 strings (1st position), distinguishes apoyando / tirando, plays a whole easy étude (Carulli, Sor).
  • Accompaniment track exit: accompanies a whole song without stopping in ballad (Boston), bolero or slow rock style; uses an alternating bass.

Level 3 — Intermediate (Trung cấp)

  • Entry: meets the Level 2 exit requirements; can play the F barre chord reasonably cleanly.
  • Core: full barre chords, legato (slurs), major – minor scales, performing on camera.
  • Classical track exit: plays in different positions, controls dynamics and tone colour, one Tárrega piece and Romance (Anónimo).
  • Accompaniment track exit: applied harmony (capo, transposition), bossa nova, chachacha, rumba, fingerstyle accompaniment.

Level 4 — Advanced (Nâng cao)

  • Entry: meets the Level 3 exit requirements.
  • Core: rasgueado, harmonics, arranging solos of Vietnamese songs, basic improvisation, playing in a band, home recording.
  • Classical track exit: tremolo, high positions and half barre, one advanced piece for the recital.
  • Accompaniment track exit: extended chords (7, 9, 11, 13), accompanying a singer (following the voice, intro / outro, recovering when the singer comes in wrong).
  • Graduation: a recital, live online or pre-recorded video, 10–15 minutes.

How the tracks split

  • Every lesson has a track label: chung (core, everyone), co-dien (Classical) or dem-hat (Accompaniment).
  • Level 1: almost entirely chung; lesson 10 (đệm hát — accompanying singing) and lesson 11 (classical) give students a taste of both.
  • From Level 2: students choose a main track and take all chung lessons + the lessons of their track.
  • Free sessions (from skipping the other track's lessons) are used for: review, practising songs / pieces of their choice, or taking lessons from the other track if they like.
  • Switching tracks: allowed at the end of each level; the teacher adds 1–2 "bridging" sessions if needed.
  • Guitar type: the Classical track needs a nylon-string guitar; the Accompaniment track works on nylon or steel-string (acoustic).
