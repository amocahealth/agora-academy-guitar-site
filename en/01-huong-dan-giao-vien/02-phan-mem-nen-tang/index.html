Software and platforms

Setting up Zoom for instruments, the limits of Google Meet, using OBS, Zalo and helper apps.

Objectives

  • The guitar sound travels over the internet as faithfully as possible: sustained notes are not cut off, nothing is squashed.
  • Pick the right tool for each job: live teaching, messaging, storing homework.

Zoom (recommended)

  • Turn on "Original sound for musicians":
    • Settings → Audio → Audio profile → choose "Original sound for musicians".
    • Older Zoom versions: tick "Show in-meeting option to enable Original sound".
    • During the lesson: click the "Original sound: Off" button in the top-left corner to switch it On.
  • Sub-options:
    • "High-fidelity music mode" — higher quality, needs a good connection.
    • Turn off "Echo cancellation" — only when wearing headphones, otherwise you get feedback.
    • "Stereo audio" — only when using a two-channel audio interface.
  • Turn off "Automatically adjust microphone volume" so the guitar does not keep getting louder and softer.
  • Why it matters: by default Zoom treats long sustained guitar notes as "noise" and cuts them, and quiet notes get swallowed.
  • Students must turn it on too — send step-by-step screenshots via Zalo (messaging app) before the first lesson.
  • Account: the free plan limits each meeting to 40 minutes → a paid account is needed for 60-minute lessons.
  • Other useful features: pin / spotlight the student's video, record the lesson (ask permission first), waiting room.

Google Meet

  • Pros: runs in the browser with nothing to install; students with a Gmail account can join.
  • Free account limits: 1-on-1 calls up to 24 hours; groups of 3 or more up to 60 minutes.
  • No instrument audio mode equivalent to Zoom's (at the time of writing) → the guitar is easily filtered and cut off.
  • If there is a "Noise cancellation" option in settings → turn it off when teaching.
  • Second camera: join again from a phone in "Companion mode", or join with mic + speaker off.
  • Use it when: the student cannot install Zoom, for short check-ins, for placement assessments.

Zalo

  • Zalo (Vietnam's main messaging app) is used for: messages, class groups, sending homework videos, quick voice-message feedback.
  • Do not use Zalo video calls for the main lesson: the audio is heavily processed and hard to control.
  • Zalo compresses videos when sending → if you need good quality, send as a "File" or via Google Drive.

OBS Studio (free, advanced)

  • Combine two cameras in one frame (picture-in-picture), add text (chord names), chord diagrams, sheet music.
  • Feed it into Zoom / Meet via the "Virtual Camera".
  • Suggested scenes: "Wide shot", "Left-hand close-up", "Right-hand close-up", "Sheet music / diagram"; assign hotkeys to switch quickly.
  • Use OBS to record demonstration videos and backing tracks for students.
  • Note: needs a reasonably powerful computer; test thoroughly before using it in a real lesson.

Apps for teaching and practice

  • Tuning: GuitarTuna, Pano Tuner, gStrings.
  • Metronome: Pro Metronome, Soundbrenner, or search "metronome" on Google.
  • Notation / tab: MuseScore (free), TuxGuitar, Guitar Pro.
  • Backing tracks / chord loops: iReal Pro, GarageBand, BandLab.
  • Slowing down recordings: 0.75× speed on YouTube, Transcribe!, Anytune.
  • Storage: Google Drive — one folder per student.

Low-latency apps (for reference)

  • SonoBus, JamKazam… allow near-simultaneous playing, but need a wired connection, short distances and complicated setup.
  • Not recommended for typical students; for alternative teaching methods see Lesson structure.
