Communicating with Vietnamese students

Draft

Vietnamese student mindset, how to ask – praise – correct, using Zalo, working with parents, schedule rules.

Understanding your students

  • Many students are reluctant to say "I don't understand" — afraid of wasting the teacher's time or being judged.
  • The answer "Dạ, em hiểu rồi ạ" ("Yes, I understand") is often just politeness and does not mean they have understood.
  • Respect for the teacher → they rarely ask again, and rarely say when the homework is too hard or their hands hurt.
  • Shy about turning on the camera or playing in front of others (especially in group classes and teenagers).
  • Working adults: busy, likely to quit if they see no progress for 2–3 weeks.

Replace "Hiểu chưa?" ("Got it?") with asking them to try

  • "Play this part again for me."
  • "Show me which fret your 2nd finger goes on."
  • "Count the beat out loud while you strum, so I can hear."
  • Multiple-choice questions are easier to answer than open ones: "What's hardest: changing chords, the right hand, or keeping time?"
  • Rating scale: "From 1 to 5, how confident are you with this part?" — below 4 means more practice.
  • Normalise mistakes: "Playing wrong is good — I need to hear the mistakes to help you."

Forms of address (xưng hô)

  • In the first lesson, ask how the student would like to be addressed.
  • Suggestions: thầy – em (teacher – younger student); anh / chú – em, or anh / chị (for students older than the teacher); thầy – con or thầy – em (children).
  • Keep forms of address consistent in class and in the Zalo (messaging app) group.

Praising and correcting

  • Formula: specific praise → correct one thing → encourage.
    • Example: "Your right hand is more even than last week. Now try keeping your 3rd finger closer to the fret. Get that and this piece is done!"
  • Give specific praise ("C to G right on the beat") rather than generic praise ("great").
  • Correct only one mistake at a time; note the others in your log for the next lesson.
  • Praise effort and progress, not just talent.
  • Avoid comparing with other students.

Motivation: familiar Vietnamese songs

  • At each level, students choose a "target song" they love to be able to play by the end of the level.
  • Mix familiar songs into technical work: e.g. use the chord progression of a pop song to practise chord changes.
  • Suggested sources: Trịnh Công Sơn songs ("Diễm xưa"), bolero ("Duyên phận"), pop ("Nơi này có anh"), children's songs ("Cả nhà thương nhau"), folk songs ("Bèo dạt mây trôi").
  • Classical track: include solo guitar arrangements of folk songs and Vietnamese music.
  • Use only song titles + chord progressions; no lyrics in the materials. List: Song list.

Zalo

  • Private students: 1-on-1 chat; group classes: a separate Zalo group for each class.
  • Pin messages: schedule, fixed Zoom link, class rules, Google Drive folder link.
  • Use a poll ("Bình chọn") to settle make-up lesson times for group classes.
  • Set reply hours (e.g. 8:00–21:00) and tell students clearly.
  • Short voice messages (30") often work better than long text messages.

Working with parents (young students)

  • First lesson: a parent sits in, helps place the device, tune the guitar and install Zoom.
  • Children under 10: a parent helps with tuning and reminds them to practise 10–15 minutes a day.
  • A separate Zalo group: teacher – parent (– child if old enough); a short report each month.
  • Guidance for parents: praise effort, don't force long practice, don't compare with "other people's children".
  • Check guitar size: young children need a 1/2 or 3/4 guitar — a guitar that is too big causes bad posture and sore hands.

Punctuality and scheduling (suggestions — the school sets the final policy)

  • Write the rules as a short document, send it via Zalo and pin it from the first lesson.
  • Cancelled at least 24 hours ahead: a make-up lesson is given.
  • Late cancellation / no notice: counted as taken.
  • Arriving late: the lesson still ends on time; more than 15' late → counted as a no-show.
  • Teacher cancels: always give a make-up lesson, with as much notice as possible.
  • Send reminders via Zalo 1 day or 1 hour before (especially in the first weeks).
  • Flexible within limits: traffic jams, rush-hour flooding → allow up to 5' late.
  • Announce breaks for Tết (Lunar New Year) and public holidays in advance.
