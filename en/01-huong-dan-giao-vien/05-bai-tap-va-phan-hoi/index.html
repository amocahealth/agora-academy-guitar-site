Homework and feedback

Draft

Assigning clear homework, showing students how to film with a phone, giving time-stamped feedback.

Objectives

  • Students practise correctly and regularly between lessons.
  • The teacher hears better-quality guitar sound than over Zoom (a recorded video avoids live audio compression).
  • A video "portfolio" lets students see their own progress.

Principles for assigning homework

  • Specific and measurable: tempo (bpm), repetitions, duration — never "practise this until it's smooth".
  • Short: 15–30 minutes a day in total; children 10–15 minutes.
  • Three parts: warm-up / technique (5') → new material (10') → a favourite piece (5–15').
  • One video submission per week, 30 seconds – 2 minutes, of exactly the part the teacher asked for.
  • Send the homework in writing via Zalo (messaging app) right after the lesson (students often forget spoken instructions).

Homework template

  • "Warm-up: fingers 1-2-3-4 on each string, 60 bpm, 3 minutes."
  • "Technique: change Am ↔ C, 4 beats per chord, 70 bpm, 5 times without stumbling."
  • "Piece: ballad strumming pattern, progression C – Am – F – G, 2 rounds in a row."
  • "Video to submit: the piece, send before Thursday evening."

Showing students how to film

  • Place the phone horizontally and keep it still (clamp mount or propped against a stack of books).
  • The frame shows both hands and the neck; for children, the whole body so posture can be checked.
  • Phone about 1 m from the guitar; a quiet room, fan off, TV off.
  • No filters, no editing, no cuts — film in one continuous take.
  • Say the lesson and tempo at the start of the video ("Lesson 7, 70 bpm").
  • File name when uploading to Drive: Ten-HocVien_Bai07_2026-11-20.mp4 (student name, lesson number, date).
  • Send via Zalo (quick) or Google Drive (keeps better quality).

Time-stamped feedback

  • Reply within 48 hours; any later and students easily lose motivation.
  • Structure:
    • "Good points: even right hand, steady time."
    • "0:12 — C chord buzzes on string 2: arch finger 1 more."
    • "0:45 — slightly late changing to G: get finger 3 ready earlier."
    • "Priority this week: only fix 0:12."
  • Lots of mistakes → note only the 1–2 most important; leave the rest for later.
  • You can reply with a voice message, or a 20" video demonstrating the spot to fix.
  • A mistake repeated over several weeks → bring it into the "Review" part of the next lesson.

Storage and tracking

  • Google Drive: one folder per student, with subfolders per level.
  • Keep the start-of-level and end-of-level videos for a "before – after" comparison — very motivating.
  • Progress log (spreadsheet): date, lesson, tempo reached, main mistakes, notes.
  • Students keep a practice diary: see Practice plan template.

When a student does not submit homework

  • Send one gentle reminder via Zalo, without blame.
  • Ask why: busy, homework too hard, shy about filming?
  • Reduce the load: "Just film 30 seconds of the easiest part."
  • Shy about filming → allow an audio recording first, then move gradually to video.
  • No submissions for several weeks → have a private talk (with the parent for children) about goals and schedule.
