Lesson videos (2): Editing and publishing

Draft

Editing software, a multi-camera editing workflow, adding chord diagrams and tab, audio processing, subtitles, export settings, putting videos on lesson pages and quality checks.

Objectives

  • Teachers can edit a lesson video from the footage they have shot (Lesson videos (1)).
  • The video has visual aids (chord diagrams, tab, finger numbers) that match the lesson page.
  • The video is exported with the right settings, put in the right place, and checked before it reaches students.

The 7-step editing workflow

1. Organise your files

  • One folder per video: cap-1/bai-08/raw/ (original footage), project/ (editing project), export/ (exported files).
  • Rename the original files after the slate you read while filming: c06-lan2-qua-vai.mp4 (scene 6, take 2, over the shoulder).
  • Back up the original footage (Google Drive / external drive) before you start editing.

2. Sync the camera angles and audio

  • Line the files up on the timeline, matching them by the clap (the spike in the waveform) at the start of each shot.
  • DaVinci Resolve: select the clips → Auto Sync Audio → Based on Waveform; then create a Multicam clip.
  • Use the audio from the good mic (audio interface) as the main track; mute the cameras' audio.

3. Rough cut

  • Cut the excess: slates, claps, failed takes, long silences.
  • Keep the script's structure order (open with music → objectives → break it down → mistakes → play along → summary).
  • Target length: skill videos 1–3', full-lesson videos 5–10'. Longer → split into Part 1, Part 2.

4. Switching camera angles

  • Change angle when the content changes: talking → wide shot; showing fingers → over the shoulder / left-hand close-up; talking about strumming → right-hand close-up.
  • Hold each angle for at least 3–4 seconds — cutting too fast makes learners lose track of the fingers.
  • Don't cut in the middle of a musical phrase while demonstrating: switch angle at the start of a bar or during a rest.
  • Picture-in-picture: a large wide shot + a small left-hand close-up inset (or the other way round) for the play-along part — students see both posture and fingers.
  • Wrong – right split screen: put 2 clips side by side, labelled "WRONG" in red / "RIGHT" in green ("SAI" / "ĐÚNG" in Vietnamese-language videos) — very effective for the common-mistakes part.

5. Visual aids (graphics)

  • Chord diagrams and tab: take screenshots straight from the lesson page (the site already draws the diagrams as SVG) → the video and the lesson page are identical. Tip: open the page in the light colour theme, zoom the browser to 200%, and capture the diagram area.
  • Put graphics in the 1/3 of the frame left empty while filming; never cover the hands.
  • Finger numbers / string names: a small circled number right next to the finger (shown for 2–3 seconds) when explaining a fingering.
  • Section titles (lower third): "Step 2 — Changing Am → C" ("Bước 2 — Chuyển Am → C" in Vietnamese-language videos) in the bottom corner; the same font and colour in every video.
  • On-screen count: "1 – 2 – 3 – 4" flashing on the beat during the play-along part.
  • Slow motion: 60 fps footage → 50% speed; mark it clearly with "× 0.5" in a corner of the screen. Don't slow down sections with sound (the audio distorts) — mute the slowed section or lay in audio of you really playing slowly.
  • Repeat the hard bit: show the hardest 3–5 seconds 2–3 times in a row.
  • On-screen text must be readable on a phone: text height ≥ 5% of the frame height, high contrast, at most 1 line at a time.

6. Audio

  • Voice: light noise reduction (using the "room tone" you recorded), EQ cut below 80 Hz, light compression to even it out.
  • Guitar: no heavy noise reduction or heavy compression — they kill the sustain and dynamics; only cut below 60–80 Hz if it's boomy.
  • Balance: voice and guitar should sound equally loud; viewers shouldn't have to adjust the volume mid-video.
  • Overall loudness: about -14 LUFS (the YouTube standard); peaks no higher than -1 dBTP.
  • No background music under demos or explanations — it masks the guitar. Background music only for short intros / outros, and it must be licensed.

7. Subtitles

  • Vietnamese subtitles on every video — many students watch on their phones in public places with the sound off.
  • Use automatic subtitles (CapCut, YouTube), then correct the terminology by hand: chord names (Am, C), finger numbers, "p-i-m-a", "apoyando"…
  • English version: translate the .srt subtitle file — no need to reshoot the video.

ffmpeg -i ban-xuat.mov -c:v libx264 -crf 23 -preset slow -vf "scale=-2:720" \
       -c:a aac -b:a 160k -movflags +faststart cap-1/bai-08/ngon-tru-am-c.mp4
  • -movflags +faststart lets the video start playing while it is still downloading; -crf 23 balances quality and file size (lower number = better looking, bigger file).
  • Audio files (type = "audio"): MP3 192 kbps or M4A/AAC 160 kbps.

Putting videos into the curriculum

Each media block on a lesson page is a "placeholder" waiting for a video. There are 2 ways to fill it:

  1. File in assets/ — save the file at the exact src path. Example block: 
    ```media
type = "video"
src = "cap-1/bai-08/ngon-tru-am-c.mp4"
caption = "Am → C with an anchor finger; changing C → D and D → G"
```
    → save the file as assets/cap-1/bai-08/ngon-tru-am-c.mp4. The yellow "Media to be added" box automatically turns into a video player.
  2. YouTube (unlisted) — upload in YouTube Studio, change the block to type = "youtube" and src = "<video ID>" (the part after v= in the link). Good for long videos and full-lesson videos; saves the site's bandwidth.
  • Private videos (recordings of lessons showing students, students' homework) never go on YouTube, not even unlisted — keep them on the class Google Drive.
  • After adding the video: run cargo run -- report to check that the lesson has no missing media left; change the lesson's status from draft → review so another teacher can look it over.

YouTube titles and descriptions

  • Title: Agora Guitar · Level 1 · Lesson 8 · Am → C with an anchor finger (for Vietnamese-language videos: Agora Guitar · Cấp 1 · Bài 8 · Chuyển Am → C bằng ngón trụ).
  • Description: link to the lesson page, objectives, homework, timestamps (chapters):
    • The first line must be 0:00; at least 3 timestamps, each section ≥ 10 seconds long → YouTube splits the video into chapters automatically.
    • Example: 0:00 The lesson's end result · 0:20 Objectives · 1:00 Fretting the C chord · 4:00 Common mistakes · 5:00 Play along at 60 – 70 bpm.
  • One playlist per level, in lesson order.
  • Thumbnail: the same template for every video — a large lesson number, a short skill name, a close-up of hands on the guitar.

Quality check before publishing

Watch the whole video on a phone, once with headphones and once through the phone's speaker.

  • Opens with music within the first 20 seconds; no long introduction.
  • The objectives in the video match the objectives on the lesson page.
  • The left-hand fingers are clearly visible whenever a fingering is explained.
  • Diagrams / tab in the video are the same as on the lesson page (finger numbers, x/o symbols).
  • The guitar is in tune (check with a tuner app on the demo sections).
  • Voice and guitar are balanced; no metronome, fan or motorbike noise bleeding in.
  • Subtitles use the correct terms and don't cover the graphics.
  • A call to action at least every 2–3 minutes; there is a play-along part.
  • No copyrighted music (songs, unlicensed background music).
  • No student images / names without written consent (children: parental consent).
  • Another teacher has watched it and given feedback (the review step).

Improving after publishing

  • YouTube Analytics → Audience retention: where the graph drops sharply = a confusing or boring section; where it spikes = students rewinding to watch again → a hard section that may need its own video.
  • Note down questions students keep asking after watching the video → add the answers to the video or the lesson page.
  • Fix a video by reshooting individual shots, not the whole video — that is why you film shot by shot and keep the editing project.
  • Every term: pick the 3 videos with the lowest completion rates and remake them.

Realistic time and division of labour

  • Beginners: 1 minute of finished video ≈ 30–60 minutes of work (script, filming, editing, checking). With experience: 15–20 minutes.
  • Batch the work: one filming day for 4–6 videos from the same level (same camera setup, same lighting); edit on other days.
  • Division of labour in a centre: teachers handle content and demonstrations; one person (or a freelancer) handles editing and graphics using the shared template.
  • Film these first: the goal videos and skill videos for Level 1 (what new students need most and what gets watched most).

Common mistakes

  • Too many transitions and flying text → visually cluttered, distracts from the hands.
  • Graphics covering the fingers or the subtitles.
  • Background music under the demonstrations.
  • Exporting a 2 GB 4K file for a 3-minute video → students can't download it on 4G.
  • Publishing a public video showing students without asking permission.
  • Every teacher using different fonts, colours and structures → the course looks unprofessional.
