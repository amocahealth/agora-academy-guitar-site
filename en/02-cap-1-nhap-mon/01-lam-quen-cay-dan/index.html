Lesson 1: Getting to know the guitar

Core⏱ 60 minDraft

Parts of the guitar, classical vs. acoustic, choosing and caring for a guitar, setting up the online class.

Objectives

  • Students correctly name the main parts of the guitar.
  • Students can tell a classical (nylon-string) from an acoustic (steel-string) guitar and know which track their guitar suits.
  • Students know how to care for a guitar in Vietnam's climate.
  • The student's camera and microphone are set up correctly for the whole course.

Preparation

  • Teacher: 1 classical + 1 acoustic guitar (if available) for comparison; a labelled diagram of the guitar to screen-share.
  • Student: their guitar, a cleaning cloth, phone + stand; a second device for close-ups if available.

Main content

  • Parts of the guitar: headstock, tuning pegs, nut, neck, frets, body, sound hole, bridge.
  • Nylon-string classical guitar: wide neck, soft strings, easier on the fingers — suits the Classical track and children.
    • Common in Vietnam: Yamaha C40/C70, handmade Vietnamese classical guitars (about VND 1–3 million).
  • Steel-string acoustic guitar: slimmer neck, bright and loud — suits đệm hát (singing with guitar accompaniment).
    • Common: Yamaha F310/F370, Rosen, Vietnamese-made acoustics (about VND 1–3 million).
  • Never put steel strings on a classical guitar (cracked neck, bridge pulls off).
  • Quick check for cheap guitars: action (string height) — if the strings are too high it really hurts; have a shop adjust the nut and bridge.
  • Care:
    • Northern humid season (mùa nồm) / southern rainy season: keep the guitar in its bag with silica gel packs.
    • Keep it away from sunny windows, direct air-conditioning, and car boots.
    • Wipe the strings after playing (sweat makes steel strings rust quickly).
  • Carrying the guitar: hold it by the neck, not by the strap.

Lesson flow (60 minutes)

  • 10' — Greetings, find out the student's goals (classical / accompaniment / undecided) and musical background
  • 15' — Set up camera and audio, test how the guitar sounds over the connection
  • 20' — Parts of the guitar, compare the 2 types, check the student's guitar on camera
  • 10' — Guitar care; teacher plays a short piece to inspire
  • 5' — Summary, assign homework, schedule the next session

Online teaching notes

  • Main camera: at the student's chest height, 1.5–2 m away, showing the whole body head to toe + the whole guitar.
  • Second camera (if available): a second phone joins the call with mic and speaker off to avoid feedback.
  • Turn off noise suppression ("Original Sound for Musicians" in Zoom); test by having the student pluck one string and let it ring — if the sound is cut off, suppression is still on.
  • Show the student how to turn off camera mirroring so left/right don't get confused.
  • Ask the student to bring the headstock and bridge close to the camera, and sight along the neck → the teacher checks string height and neck bow.
  • Children: ask a parent to be present for the first 10' to set up devices.
  • Record the setup details (devices, placement) in the student's profile.

Common mistakes

  • Buying a guitar too big for the player (children on a 4/4 size).
  • Cheap guitar with very high action → student quits because it hurts; get it set up before lessons.
  • Leaving the guitar leaning against a wall without a case — it falls over and gets damp and mouldy.
  • Camera too close, showing only the student's face.

Homework

  • Take 3 photos of the guitar (front, headstock, along the neck) and send them to the teacher via Zalo (Vietnamese messaging app).
  • Name the 8 parts of the guitar out loud, record a 30-second video.
  • Install GuitarTuna or Pano Tuner and a metronome app.
  • Set up a fixed practice spot: chair without armrests, phone stand.

Completion criteria

  • Correctly names at least 6/8 parts of the guitar.
  • Knows whether their guitar is classical or acoustic, and why it was chosen.
  • Camera shows the full body + guitar; the guitar sound isn't cut off by noise suppression.
  • Tuner and metronome apps installed.
