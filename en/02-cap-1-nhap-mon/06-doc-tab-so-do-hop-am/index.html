Lesson 6: Reading tab and chord diagrams

Core⏱ 60 minDraft

Reading tab (tablature) and chord diagrams, x/o symbols, finger numbers.

Objectives

  • Students read and play a simple line of tab (without rhythm).
  • Students read a chord diagram: strings, frets, finger numbers, x and o symbols.
  • Students read chord symbols on a song sheet (Am, C, G7...) the way Vietnamese song sheets usually write them.

Preparation

  • Teacher: a PDF of sample tab and chord diagrams to screen-share; a virtual whiteboard (Zoom Whiteboard) or paper + camera.
  • Student: guitar, pen and paper; the lesson PDF printed or open on a computer/tablet.

Main content

  • Tab (tablature):
    • 6 lines = 6 strings; the top line is string 1 (the thinnest), the bottom line is string 6.
    • The number = the fret to press; 0 = open string.
    • Read left to right; numbers stacked vertically = played together.
    • Basic tab doesn't show note lengths — you need a recording or added rhythm notation.
  • Reading example (an exercise, not a song):
  • Chord diagram:
    • Vertical lines = strings (string 6 on the left → string 1 on the right); horizontal lines = fret wires; the thick line at the top = the nut.
    • Dot = where to fret; the number in the dot = left-hand finger.
    • o = play the open string; x = don't play that string.
Reading example: Em and C (number = left-hand finger)
  • Chord symbols: capital letter = major (C = C major); add m = minor (Am = A minor); 7 = seventh chord (covered in Level 2).
  • When looking up chords online (hopamchuan.com, Vietnamese chord sites): check the key and capo before using them.

Lesson flow (60 minutes)

  • 5' — Warm-up, tuning
  • 10' — Review the 1-2-3-4 exercise, watch the student's video
  • 20' — Reading tab: screen-share, read each note aloud ("string 1, fret 3")
  • 15' — Reading chord diagrams: try placing the fingers from the Em diagram (no chord changes yet)
  • 5' — 5-question mini quiz, assign homework
  • 5' — Buffer

Online teaching notes

  • Screen-share tab in large print on a white background; small text is hard to read on a phone → send the PDF beforehand via Zalo (Vietnamese messaging app).
  • Use the pointer / annotation pen on the shared screen to point at each note being read.
  • Check understanding with an oral quiz: the teacher points at a number in the tab, the student says "which string, which fret" — no playing needed, so latency is not an issue.
  • When the student places their fingers from a diagram: left-hand close-up, the teacher compares it with the diagram on screen.
  • Orientation: a chord diagram looks like the neck standing upright in front of you — explain by having the student hold the guitar upright in front of the camera.

Common mistakes

  • Reading tab upside down (thinking the top line is string 6 because string 6 is "on top" when holding the guitar).
  • Taking tab numbers as finger numbers (they are fret numbers).
  • Taking chord diagram numbers as fret numbers (they are finger numbers).
  • Strumming strings marked x.

Homework

  • Play the example tab line above 5 times, saying the string–fret aloud before playing.
  • Write tab for any 4 notes, send a photo to the teacher with a video of playing it.
  • Read the Em and Am diagrams in the PDF and take a photo of the hand placed from the diagrams.

Completion criteria

  • Reads 5/5 tab notes correctly when the teacher points at random.
  • Explains what x, o and the finger numbers mean on a chord diagram.
  • Distinguishes "number on tab = fret" from "number on diagram = finger".
