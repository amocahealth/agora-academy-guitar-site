Level 1: Beginner · Lesson 7/17

Lesson 7: Em and Am chords

Core⏱ 60 minDraft

The first two chords: E minor (Em) and A minor (Am), even downstrums and changing chords.

Objectives

  • Students fret Em and Am correctly with every string ringing clearly.
  • Students strum 4 even downstrokes on each chord.
  • Students change Em ↔ Am within 4 beats at a slow tempo (60).

Preparation

  • Teacher: Em and Am diagrams to screen-share; a metronome app.
  • Student: guitar in tune, a pick (Accompaniment track), a metronome app, the chord diagram PDF from lesson 6.

Main content

  • Em (E minor): finger 2 on string 5, fret 2; finger 3 on string 4, fret 2; strum all 6 strings.
  • Am (A minor): finger 1 on string 2, fret 1; finger 2 on string 4, fret 2; finger 3 on string 3, fret 2; don't play string 6 (x).
Em and Am — fingerings used in this lesson
  • Em → Am tip: keep the finger 2–3 shape, move the pair over by one string (toward string 1), then add finger 1.
  • Check each string: hold the chord, pluck strings 6 → 1 one at a time; fix the finger on any string that buzzes or is muted.
  • Downstrum (↓): with the thumb, the back of the index finger, or a pick; strum from the lowest string in the chord to string 1.
  • Chord-change practice:
    • "Place – lift": place the chord, lift the hand into the air, place it again — 10 times per chord.
    • One-minute changes: count how many Em ↔ Am changes you make in 60 seconds, log the result every day.
    • With the metronome at 60: 4 beats Em → 4 beats Am; at first it's OK to drop beat 4 to make the change in time.

Lesson flow (60 minutes)

  • 5' — Warm-up, tuning
  • 10' — Review chord diagrams, look at the student's photos of their hand placed from the diagrams
  • 20' — Em: fret, check each string, strum; then the same for Am
  • 20' — Em ↔ Am changes: place–lift, one-minute changes, then with the metronome
  • 5' — Summary, log the first one-minute result, assign homework

Online teaching notes

  • Left-hand close-up to check finger placement; the teacher must see whether the fingertips touch the neighbouring strings.
  • Checking clarity over the call: ask the student to pluck one string at a time, slowly (not strum) — single strings are easier to hear than a compressed strum.
  • Metronome: the student runs the metronome on their own device (not the teacher's metronome over the call — it will drift).
    • One earbud in, or the phone close to the guitar; it's fine if the call mic doesn't pick up the click.
  • The teacher times the one-minute changes and counts them on camera — this exercise is unaffected by latency.
  • Give specific praise ("finger 3 is more curved now") because online students get discouraged easily when they can't see progress.

Common mistakes

  • Strumming string 6 on Am → muddy sound.
  • Finger 1 on Am touching string 1 → string 1 muted.
  • Placing one finger at a time when changing (slow) instead of the whole hand shape at once.
  • Stopping the right hand to wait for the left → losing the beat; keep the right hand steady and accept a few buzzy beats at first.
  • Staring at the left hand and hunching over.

Homework

  • Every day: check each string of Em and Am (2 minutes).
  • One-minute changes Em ↔ Am, log the count in a practice notebook; target ≥ 20 changes/minute after 1 week.
  • Metronome at 60: 4 beats Em – 4 beats Am, 8 rounds without stopping.
  • Record a 1-minute video (left-hand close-up + audible sound) and send it to the teacher.

Completion criteria

  • Em and Am: at least 5/6 (Em) and 4/5 (Am) strings ring clearly.
  • One-minute changes: ≥ 15.
  • Strums 4 even beats at tempo 60, changing chords without stopping for more than 1 beat.
