Lesson 9: Rhythm and the metronome

Core⏱ 60 minDraft

Beats and bars, 4/4 and 3/4 time, quarter notes and eighth notes, practising with a metronome.

Objectives

  • Students distinguish the beat from the bar (measure) and can count 4/4 and 3/4 out loud.
  • Students clap and play quarter notes and eighth notes in time with a metronome.
  • Students use a metronome app on their own: set the tempo, choose the number of beats, accent the downbeat.

Preparation

  • Teacher: metronome app; a simple rhythm chart (screen-shared).
  • Student: metronome app (Pro Metronome, Soundbrenner, or search "metronome" on Google), one earbud if available.

Main content

  • Beat: a steady pulse like footsteps; tempo = beats per minute (BPM).
  • 4/4 time: 4 beats per bar, beat 1 strong — count "1 – 2 – 3 – 4".
  • 3/4 time: 3 beats per bar — count "1 – 2 – 3" (waltz; many Vietnamese trữ tình (lyrical ballad) songs use 3/4).
  • Basic note values:
    • Whole note = 4 beats · half note = 2 beats · quarter note = 1 beat · eighth note = ½ beat (count "1 and 2 and...").
  • Count out loud while playing — don't skip this, even with adults.
  • Using the metronome:
    • Start at 60 BPM; only go up 4–5 BPM after playing it right 3 times in a row.
    • Turn on the beat 1 accent to hear the start of each bar.
  • Exercises (in order):
    1. Clap quarter notes with the metronome, counting 4/4 out loud.
    2. Clap eighth notes ("1 and 2 and").
    3. Pluck open string 6 with the thumb: quarter notes, then eighth notes.
    4. Strum Em: 4 quarter notes/bar → 8 eighth notes/bar (↓↑↓↑↓↑↓↑).
    5. 3/4 time: strum G, accenting beat 1, 3 beats/bar.

Lesson flow (60 minutes)

  • 5' — Warm-up, tuning
  • 10' — Review the lesson 8 progressions, watch the video the student sent
  • 20' — Beat, tempo, 4/4, 3/4, quarter and eighth notes; how to set up the metronome
  • 20' — Clapping → open strings → strumming chords with the metronome (exercises 1–5)
  • 5' — Summary, assign homework

Online teaching notes

  • Never have the student play along to the teacher's metronome over the call — 100–300 ms of latency throws the timing off.
  • The student runs the metronome on their own device; the teacher hears the click and the guitar arriving with the same delay → you can still judge whether the student is with the click.
    • If the mic doesn't pick up the click: put the metronome phone near the mic, or use the flashing-light mode and point it at the camera.
  • Judging rhythm from a pre-recorded video is more accurate than live — ask for videos with the click audible.
  • Clapping can be cut out by noise suppression → check the "Original Sound" setting again.
  • Rhythm call-and-response: the teacher claps one bar → the student claps it back; keep changing the pattern like a game (works especially well with children).

Common mistakes

  • Speeding up on easy passages, slowing down at chord changes.
  • Not counting out loud, just "feeling it" → off the beat after a few bars.
  • Setting the tempo too fast from the start.
  • Upstrums (↑) as loud as downstrums, losing the accent.
  • Treating the metronome as "the enemy" and switching it off after a mistake.

Homework

  • 5 minutes a day clapping + counting out loud with the metronome (4/4 and 3/4).
  • G – Em – C – D progression: strum 8 eighth notes/bar (↓↑), tempo 60, 4 rounds.
  • 3/4 time: strum C – G – G – C, 3 beats/bar, accent beat 1.
  • Record a video with the metronome clearly audible and send it to the teacher.

Completion criteria

  • Counts 4/4 and 3/4 out loud correctly when asked.
  • Claps quarter and eighth notes in time with the metronome at 60 for 4 bars.
  • Strums one chord progression (↓↑) at tempo 60 without speeding up.
  • Sets the tempo and number of beats in the app on their own.
