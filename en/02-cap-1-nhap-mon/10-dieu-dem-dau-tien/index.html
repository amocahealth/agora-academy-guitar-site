Level 1: Beginner · Lesson 10/17

Lesson 10: First strumming pattern

Accompaniment track⏱ 60 minDraft

Down–up strumming patterns, a basic pop strum, accompanying a whole 3–4 chord song.

Objectives

  • Students play 3 strumming patterns evenly, from easy to harder.
  • Students keep the right hand moving steadily (continuous down–up), including on "missed" strums.
  • Students accompany a whole 3–4 chord song, humming or counting out loud while they strum.

Preparation

  • Teacher: strumming pattern chart to screen-share; 2–3 suggested songs with key and chords already checked (using only Em, Am, C, G, D).
  • Student: a thin pick (0.46–0.6 mm strums easily), metronome, a song they like picked in advance so the teacher can check whether it is suitable.

Main content

  • The pendulum right hand principle: ↓ on the beat, ↑ on the "and"; when the pattern has no strum, the hand still moves but doesn't touch the strings (ghost strum).
  • Downstrum from a relaxed wrist; the upstrum only needs to catch the 3–4 thinnest strings.
  • Strumming patterns (4/4, count "1 and 2 and 3 and 4 and"):

  • Suggested songs (title + chords only; teacher double-checks before the session):
    • Happy Birthday — 3/4 time, key of G: G, D, C.
    • Kìa con bướm vàng ("Look, the yellow butterfly", folk song) — for children: C, G.
    • Student's own choice — many V-pop songs use G – Em – C – D or C – G – Am – Em; avoid songs that need F, Bm or tricky capo use at this level.
  • Putting the song together: play the progression with pattern A → B → C; only once it is steady, hum the melody along.
  • Notation on Vietnamese song sheets (e.g. hopamchuan): chords are written in [square brackets] right before the word where the chord changes.

Lesson flow (60 minutes)

  • 5' — Warm-up, tuning
  • 10' — Review ↓↑ strumming with the metronome (lesson 9)
  • 20' — Pendulum principle, patterns A → B → C on one chord (G), then on G – Em – C – D
  • 20' — Put one suggested song together; the student strums, the teacher listens and corrects
  • 5' — Summary, assign homework

Online teaching notes

  • The main camera shows the right hand and the whole guitar body; the teacher needs to see continuous pendulum motion, not just hear the sound.
  • Never sing or strum together over the call: the teacher plays one round → stops → the student plays it back (call-and-response).
  • If the student wants to sing along with the teacher: send a recorded accompaniment (backing track) for offline practice.
  • Strumming often sounds distorted/compressed through a phone mic: judge the hand motion from the picture and the sound from recorded videos.
  • Students being shy about singing on camera is normal in Vietnam — allow humming, counting out loud or just strumming; don't push singing at Level 1.
  • Send the strumming pattern chart as an image via Zalo (Vietnamese messaging app) so students can stick it up next to their practice spot.

Common mistakes

  • Stopping the right hand on the ghost strum → losing the rhythm of pattern C.
  • Strumming with the whole arm, tense shoulder.
  • Upstrums too hard, hitting the bass strings too.
  • Changing chords late → missing beat 1 of the new chord; tip: lift the left hand on the last ↑ (the "4 and").
  • Gripping the pick too tightly → harsh tone, pick rotates in the hand.

Homework

  • Patterns A, B, C on G – Em – C – D, tempo 60 → 70, 4 rounds per pattern.
  • Put together one whole song (Happy Birthday or a self-chosen song approved by the teacher).
  • Record a video accompanying the whole song (humming or counting is fine), send it before session 12.

Completion criteria

  • Plays pattern C for 4 rounds continuously, right hand never stopping on the ghost strum.
  • Accompanies a whole 3–4 chord song without stopping, changing chords on beat 1.
  • Keeps a steady tempo (60–70) throughout the song.
