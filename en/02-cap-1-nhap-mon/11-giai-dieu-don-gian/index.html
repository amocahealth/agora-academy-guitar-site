Level 1: Beginner · Lesson 11/17

Lesson 11: Simple melodies

Classical track⏱ 60 minDraft

Single-note melodies on strings 1–2 with alternating i–m: Ode to Joy and Lightly Row (public domain).

Objectives

  • Students play 2 short melodies on strings 1–2, alternating i–m and fretting one finger per fret.
  • Students combine reading tab with the note values they have learned (quarter notes, half notes).
  • Students get to know a singing tone (basic legato): each note rings until the next one sounds.

Preparation

  • Teacher: classical guitar; large-print tab of both pieces (PDF); a slow demo recording (tempo 60).
  • Student: guitar in tune, metronome, the tab PDF printed or open on a tablet.

Main content

  • Notes used in the pieces (1st position):
    • String 1: Mi (E) open · Fa (F) fret 1, finger 1 · Son (G) fret 3, finger 3.
    • String 2: Đô (C) fret 1, finger 1 · Rê (D) fret 3, finger 3.
  • Right hand: alternate i–m with free strokes (tirando); never pluck twice in a row with the same finger.
  • Ode to Joy (L. v. Beethoven, Symphony No. 9 — public domain), 4/4 time:
    • Mostly quarter notes; bar 4 of each phrase: dotted quarter – eighth – half note.
    • Teacher note: the dotted quarter was not taught in lesson 9 — explain briefly: the dot adds half the value (dotted quarter = 1.5 beats); counting "1 – 2 – and", the dotted quarter rings from beat 1 through the first half of beat 2, the eighth falls on the "and", the half note on beats 3–4.
  • Lightly Row (German folk song "Hänschen klein" — public domain), 4/4 time:
    • Bars 1, 2, 4: quarter – quarter – half; bar 3: 4 quarter notes; last bar: whole note.
  • Both melodies use only strings 1–2; the tab also shows the string-3 (G) line for orientation — string 3 is not played. ("Câu 1 / Câu 2" in the tab = phrase 1 / phrase 2.)
  • How to learn a melody: say the note names aloud → left hand only (no plucking) → bar by bar → join into phrases → add the metronome.
  • Encourage singing the note names ("Mi Mi Fa Son...") while playing — groundwork for reading treble-clef notation in Level 2.

Lesson flow (60 minutes)

  • 5' — Warm-up, tuning
  • 10' — Review alternating i–m and the 1-2-3-4 exercise on strings 1–2
  • 20' — Learn the notes on strings 1–2; Ode to Joy bar by bar → phrase 1 → phrase 2
  • 20' — Lightly Row: call-and-response bar by bar; whole piece with the metronome at 60
  • 5' — Summary, assign homework

Online teaching notes

  • 2 camera angles: left-hand close-up (check fingers 1–3 are right behind the fret) and right-hand close-up (check i–m alternation).
    • 1 camera: use a frame just wide enough to show both hands; ask the student to play slowly.
  • Single-note melodies come through the call very clearly — this is the best lesson for judging clean tone live.
  • Call-and-response bar by bar: the teacher plays one bar → the student plays it back; don't play at the same time, and don't accompany the student with chords over the call.
  • For a duet feel: send the student a recording of the accompaniment to play the melody over at home.
  • Screen-share the tab and use the pointer on each note while the student says the note names.

Common mistakes

  • Plucking with the same finger repeatedly (usually i).
  • Choppy sound: lifting left-hand fingers too early, notes not held for their full value.
  • Half notes held for only 1 beat → the melody sounds rushed.
  • Finger 3 playing Son (string 1, fret 3) lies flat and touches string 2.
  • Constantly switching between looking at the tab and the hands → losing the beat; memorise each phrase.

Homework

  • Ode to Joy: 3 times/day at tempo 60; once it's right 3 times in a row, move up to 66.
  • Lightly Row: memorise phrase 1, play the whole piece with the metronome.
  • Record both pieces (showing both hands) and send them before session 12.

Completion criteria

  • Plays both phrases of Ode to Joy without stopping, tempo 60.
  • Plays Lightly Row with correct note values (half notes held for the full 2 beats).
  • Alternates i–m on at least 80% of the notes.
  • Correctly names the notes Mi, Fa, Son, Đô, Rê on the fretboard.
