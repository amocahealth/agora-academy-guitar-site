Supplement 1: Warm-ups and finger exercises

CoreDraft

A 5-minute warm-up before every practice, left-hand finger independence exercises, right-hand plucking patterns, and how beginners can avoid pain and injury.

Supplement — used alongside Lessons 4–12, not counted as a separate session. The teacher assigns parts of this page as warm-ups; students practise them on their own at home.

Objectives

  • Students have a fixed 5-minute warm-up routine before every practice.
  • Students train the independence of the 4 left-hand fingers and right-hand finger alternation with exercises that need no music reading.
  • Students know the signs of normal pain and of pain that means stop.

Why warm up

  • Cold finger muscles and tendons → stiff fretting, easy to strain; 3–5 minutes of warming up makes the hands softer and more accurate.
  • A fixed warm-up helps students "switch into practice mode" quickly — especially adults practising in the evening after work.
  • Finger exercises give measurable progress (metronome tempo) → students see themselves improving every week, even when songs are still hard.

5-minute warm-up (without the guitar, 1 minute)

  • Shoulder rolls backwards 5 times, relax the neck: tilt the head left – right, 5 seconds each side.
  • Close – open the hand slowly 10 times, opening all the fingers fully.
  • Shake the wrists gently, as if flicking off water, for 10 seconds.
  • Finger taps: the thumb touches the tips of fingers 1–2–3–4 in turn, then back, 3 rounds on each hand.
  • No hard stretching, no bending fingers backwards — a warm-up is for warming, not stretching.

Left-hand exercises

1. The 1-2-3-4 exercise in 5th position (frets 5–8)

  • This is the 1-2-3-4 exercise from Lesson 5, but placed at frets 5–8: the frets are narrower → easier to reach and less tiring for beginners.
  • One finger per fret (finger 1 on fret 5, finger 2 on fret 6, finger 3 on fret 7, finger 4 on fret 8).
  • Fret close to the fret (right by the metal fret wire on the body side), using the fingertip, fingers curved as if holding a small ball.
  • Keep the previous finger on the string when placing the next one (finger 1 stays down while placing finger 2…) — builds stamina and finger independence.
  • Descending: from string 1 back to string 6, finger order 4-3-2-1 (place all 4 fingers first, then lift them one by one).
  • Tempo: start at metronome 50, one note per beat; go up 4 bpm after 3 clean rounds in a row.
  • Once 5th position is easy: move down to 3rd position (frets 3–6) → 1st position (frets 1–4).

2. The 1-3-2-4 variation (finger independence)

  • Same position, different finger order: 1-3-2-4. Fingers 3 and 4 tend to "stick together" — this exercise separates them.
  • Other variations once this is comfortable: 1-2-4-3, 2-1-3-4, 4-2-3-1. Change only 1 variation per week.

3. "The spider" — 2 strings

  • Fingers 1 and 3 fret on one string, fingers 2 and 4 on the next string → the left hand "crawls" diagonally like a spider.
  • Harder than the two exercises above — only practise it once the 1-2-3-4 exercise is clean at tempo 60.

4. Silent chords: place – lift without strumming

  • Place the whole chord (e.g. Em) all at once, hold for 2 seconds, lift just clear of the strings (1–2 cm), place it again. 10 times.
  • Do this with Em → Am → C → G → D. No right hand needed — you can practise while watching TV.
  • Purpose: the hand "remembers the shape" of each chord, preparing for one-minute changes (Lessons 7, 8).

Right-hand exercises

1. p – i – m – i on open strings

  • The thumb (p) plucks the bass strings (6, 5, then 4); i plucks string 3; m plucks string 2.
  • The thumb changes bass string every bar — fingers i and m stay on the same strings.

2. Ascending – descending arpeggio p-i-m-a-m-i

  • Preparation for Lesson 5 of Level 2 (p-i-m-a arpeggios). At Level 1 it just needs to be slow and even.
  • Once it is even: fret Am or C with the left hand and keep the same plucking pattern — the exercise becomes the first arpeggio accompaniment.

3. Muted strumming — Accompaniment track

  • Rest the left hand lightly across all 6 strings (without pressing down) → the strings make a "chk" sound, no notes.
  • Strum the pattern ↓ ↓↑ ↓↑ ↓↑ with the metronome: you hear the right-hand rhythm clearly without the left hand getting in the way.

Hand pain: normal, or time to stop?

  • Normal (keep practising, in shorter blocks):
    • Left-hand fingertips sore and red during the first 1–3 weeks → they will gradually build calluses.
    • Slightly tired forearm after 15–20 minutes of continuous practice.
  • Stop immediately, rest 1–2 days, tell the teacher:
    • Sharp pain in the wrist, elbow or left thumb.
    • Numbness or pins and needles in the fingers (especially fingers 4–5), or pain that lasts into the next day.
    • Blistered or bleeding fingertips.
  • Pain lasting more than 1 week → see a doctor; never try to "practise through the pain".
  • Prevention:
    • Practise short and often (2 × 15 minutes is better than 1 × 45 minutes) — especially in the first week.
    • Press just hard enough for the note not to buzz; try pressing gradually lighter until it buzzes, then add a little pressure — that is the right amount.
    • The left thumb sits behind the neck and does not squeeze the neck like a racket handle.
    • Every 10–15 minutes: let the hands drop, shake them gently, drink some water.
    • A guitar with high action (string height above the frets) makes it hurt twice as much → have a guitar shop adjust it (see Supplement 5).

Online teaching notes

  • Use the left-hand close-up camera to check curved fingers and fretting close to the fret; many mistakes can't be heard over Zoom but are clearly visible.
  • The teacher counts in, then mutes the mic while the student plays along to the metronome on their own device.
  • Students log the tempo reached each week in their practice notebook (Appendix 03) — the teacher looks at the numbers instead of listening to the whole exercise again.
  • Children: turn the 1-2-3-4 exercise into a game of "4 soldiers climbing the stairs", one soldier per finger.

Common mistakes

  • Fingers straight, fretting with the finger pad → touching the next string, muting it.
  • Finger 4 lifted too high (3–5 cm) when not in use → slow to put down when needed.
  • Holding the breath and raising the shoulders on hard finger exercises.
  • Practising faster than you can play → drilling mistakes into habits; slow and clean always beats fast and buzzy.
  • Skipping the warm-up when "there's no time" — the 5-minute warm-up is the part most worth keeping.

Homework

  • Every practice: 1-minute warm-up without the guitar + 4 minutes of finger exercises following the weekly schedule above.
  • Log in the practice notebook the highest tempo at which you play the 1-2-3-4 exercise cleanly 3 times.
  • Every 2 weeks: record a 30-second video of the finger exercises (left-hand close-up) and send it to the teacher.

Completion criteria

  • Does the 5-minute warm-up in the right order without looking at the instructions.
  • Plays 1-2-3-4 in 5th position up – down at tempo 60, one note per beat, without buzzing.
  • Plays p-i-m-a-m-i on open strings evenly at tempo 60.
  • Can tell normal pain from pain that means stop.
