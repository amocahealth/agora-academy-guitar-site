Supplement 2: Practising effectively at home

How to structure a 20–30 minute home practice, practise slowly with a metronome, break hard passages into chunks, keep a practice log and record homework videos for the teacher.

Supplement — the teacher introduces it at the end of Lesson 1 or Lesson 2, and brings it up again whenever a student is "practising a lot but not improving".

Objectives

  • Students know how to split a 20–30 minute practice into clear parts.
  • Students use the metronome and a "tempo ladder" to build speed in a controlled way.
  • Students keep a practice notebook and record homework videos the way the teacher asks.

"Deliberate practice" vs. "playing for fun"

  • Playing for fun: playing what you already know, start to finish, skipping over mistakes. Still needed — it keeps the joy of the guitar alive.
  • Deliberate practice:
    • Has one small, measurable goal for each practice ("change Am → C within 1 beat at tempo 60").
    • Works on exactly the hard spot, instead of replaying the whole song.
    • Has feedback: metronome, audio recordings, video, practice notebook.
    • Is slightly hard — too easy and you don't improve, too hard and you give up.
  • Every practice should have both: most of the time on deliberate practice, finishing with playing for fun.

Structure of a home practice (25 minutes)

TimePartLevel 1 example
3'Tuning + warm-upTuner app; warm-up without the guitar (Supplement 1)
4'Finger / technique exercises1-2-3-4 in 5th position, p-i-m-i
10'New material — the hardest spotC → G change; strumming pattern C; the 2 hard bars of Ode to Joy
5'Review older materialLast week's progression; one-minute changes
3'Playing for funA song you like, without stopping to fix things
  • Children: split it in two — 2 × 10–12 minutes during the day.
  • Busy days: 10 minutes still beats 0 minutes — keep part 1 + part 3.

Slow practice and the "tempo ladder"

  • The rule: slow enough to play 100% clean, and only then speed up.
  • Tempo ladder:
    1. Find the tempo at which you play it right 3 times in a row with no mistakes (e.g. 52).
    2. Go up 4 bpm (56). 3 clean rounds → go up again.
    3. 2 mistakes in a row → drop back 8 bpm, play 3 clean rounds, then finish that part.
    4. Log the highest clean tempo in the practice notebook. The next day, start 8 bpm below that number.
  • Don't force the tempo within one day — real speed gains happen between days, once the hands have "slept on it".

Breaking hard passages into chunks (chunking)

  • Find exactly where it goes wrong (usually one chord change or 2–3 notes), not "the whole song is hard".
  • Practise just that spot: the last note of the previous bar + the first note of the next bar (or the previous chord + the next chord).
  • 5 clean repetitions → add 1 bar before it → add 1 bar after it.
  • "Practise from the end" tip: learn the last phrase first, then the second-to-last phrase + the last phrase… → the ending is always the most secure part.

Practice notebook (practice log)

  • Use the template in Appendix 03 — Weekly practice plan template, or notes on your phone.
  • For each practice, log 4 things:
    • Date, number of minutes.
    • What you practised (piece, section).
    • A number: highest clean tempo, number of chord changes in 1 minute.
    • 1 sentence: what was hard today / a question for the teacher.
  • Send a photo of the practice notebook to the teacher every week via Zalo (Vietnam's main messaging app) — the teacher skims it before the lesson.

Recording homework videos for the teacher

  • Length: 30–60 seconds, only the part the teacher asked for — don't send a 10-minute video.
  • Camera angle: phone in landscape, showing the whole guitar and both hands; if the teacher asks to check the left hand, film a close-up of the neck.
  • Lighting: face the window / lamp, don't sit with the light behind you.
  • Sound: phone 50 cm – 1 m from the guitar; turn off the fan or air conditioner if it's noisy; set the metronome loud enough to hear but quieter than the guitar.
  • Before playing: say one sentence — "Lesson 8, G – Em – C – D progression, tempo 60".
  • Record at most 3 takes and send the best one — it doesn't need to be perfect; the teacher needs to see real mistakes to fix them.
  • File / message name: Ten-HocVien_Bai08_tempo60 → easy for the teacher to find again and compare.

Keeping the practice habit

  • Keep the guitar out of its case, on a stand — seeing it makes you want to pick it up.
  • Attach practice time to something you already do: after dinner, before your shower…
  • Set goals in days ("5 days/week"), not in hours.
  • Learn with a friend / family member: show each other videos at the end of the week.
  • Apartment living, evening practice: a nylon-string classical guitar is quieter; practise the left hand without strumming; use a string mute (dampener) — details in Supplement 5.

When you feel you're "not improving" (plateaus)

  • Normal — it usually happens around weeks 3–4 (sore fingers) and weeks 7–8 (chord changes not yet fast enough).
  • Look back through the practice notebook: if the tempo is still slowly rising → you are still improving, you just can't feel it yet.
  • Record a video and compare it with the week 1 video — there is almost always a clear difference.
  • Change exercises to avoid boredom, but keep the same skill (e.g. change the progression, keep the same chord change).
  • Tell the teacher — a lesson may need to be split over 2 sessions (allowed at Level 1).

Notes for teachers

  • Spend 5 minutes in Lesson 1 or Lesson 2 making a practice schedule together with the student: which days, what time, where.
  • Homework always includes a number (tempo, repetitions, rounds) so students know for themselves when they're done.
  • Parents of young students: send them the 25-minute table above and ask them to remind the child when it's practice time — don't ask parents to "correct the playing".
  • Praise the habit (practising all 5 days), not just the results.

Common mistakes

  • Starting the whole piece again after every mistake → the beginning is very secure, the end never gets practised.
  • Turning on the metronome but not listening to it — playing ahead of the click.
  • Practising 2 hours on Sunday and not touching the guitar all week.
  • Only practising songs, skipping finger exercises and chord changes.
  • Sending videos that are too long or filmed in portrait, so the teacher can't see the left hand.

Homework

  • Make a practice schedule for 1 week (days + times) and send it to the teacher.
  • Use the 25-minute structure above on at least 4 days this week, and log it in the practice notebook.
  • Apply the tempo ladder to one of this week's exercises; log the starting tempo and the end-of-week tempo.

Completion criteria

  • Has a fixed practice schedule of at least 5 days/week.
  • The practice notebook has a number (tempo / repetitions) for every practice.
  • Sends homework videos as requested: landscape, 30–60 seconds, with a spoken introduction.
