Supplement 3: Basic music theory and ear training

CoreDraft

Note names in Đô–Rê–Mi and C–D–E, whole and half steps on the fretboard, the notes in 1st position, which notes make up a chord, and ear training for beginners.

Supplement — each part takes 5–10 minutes; the teacher slots it into the warm-up of Lessons 6–12. No need to cover it all at once; the goal is to understand what you are playing, not to pass a theory exam.

Objectives

  • Students read note names in both systems: Đô Rê Mi and C D E.
  • Students know that 1 fret = a half step, and can find the natural notes in 1st position.
  • Students know which 3 notes make up each chord they have learned, and why anchor fingers make chord changes faster.
  • Students can tell major / minor chords apart and count chord changes by ear.

1. Note names: two systems

Vietnamese students learn melodies with fixed-Đô solfège (Đô Rê Mi…), while chords are written with letter names — so they need both rows:

ĐôRêMiFaSonLaSi
CDEFGAB
  • In Vietnam, melodies are usually read in Đô–Rê–Mi; chords are written with letters: C = Đô trưởng (C major), Am = La thứ (A minor).
  • Memory tip: start from La = A (the first letter) and count on: Si = B, Đô = C…
  • Names of the 6 open strings (Lesson 3): Mi – La – Rê – Son – Si – Mi = E – A – D – G – B – E (string 6 → string 1).

2. Whole steps and half steps

  • On the guitar: 1 fret = a half step, 2 frets = a whole step.
  • Between the 7 natural notes the distance is a whole step, except E–F (Mi–Fa) and B–C (Si–Đô), which are only a half step:
C ── D ── E ─ F ── G ── A ── B ─ C
   1    1   ½   1    1    1   ½
  • (1 = whole step, ½ = half step; in solfège: Đô – Rê – Mi – Fa – Son – La – Si – Đô.)
  • A note lying between two notes a whole step apart has two names: the sharp (#) of the lower note or the flat (b) of the upper note. Example: string 5, fret 1 = A# (La#) = Bb (Si b).
  • Try it on string 5: A (open) → B (fret 2) → C (fret 3) — B and C are only 1 fret apart.

3. Natural notes in 1st position

  • A note on fret 1 → finger 1, fret 2 → finger 2, fret 3 → finger 3 (the one-finger-per-fret principle from Lesson 5).
  • The C major scale in 1st position — 8 notes C D E F G A B C (Đô Rê Mi Fa Son La Si Đô):
  • Sing the note names while playing ("Đô – Rê – Mi…") — this trains the ear and prepares for reading treble-clef notation in Level 2.

4. How chords are built

  • A basic chord (a triad) has 3 notes: the root (1), and the 3rd and 5th notes counting up from the root.
    • C major: C (1) – D – E (3) – F – G (5) → C = C – E – G (Đô – Mi – Son).
  • The 5 chords of Level 1:
ChordVietnamese nameRoot (1)3rd5thCharacter
CĐô trưởng (C major)C (Đô)E (Mi)G (Son)bright, happy
GSon trưởng (G major)G (Son)B (Si)D (Rê)bright, happy
DRê trưởng (D major)D (Rê)F# (Fa#)A (La)bright, happy
AmLa thứ (A minor)A (La)C (Đô)E (Mi)sad, dark
EmMi thứ (E minor)E (Mi)G (Son)B (Si)sad, dark
  • A chord played on 6 strings still has only 3 notes — the notes are repeated in different octaves.
  • Major vs. minor comes down to the 3rd: in a major chord the 3rd is 4 frets above the root; in a minor chord, 3 frets.
  • Why the anchor finger works: Am (A–C–E) and C (C–E–G) share 2 notes, C and E → fingers 1 and 2 stay put (Lesson 8). Likewise: Em (E–G–B) and G (G–B–D) share G and B.
  • Try it on the virtual guitar 🎸: pick C, then Am, and turn on "chord tones" to see the shared notes on the fretboard.

6. Ear training — 5 short games

Each game takes 2–3 minutes; the teacher plays, the student answers — one direction only, so it works well over Zoom.

  1. Higher or lower: the teacher plays 2 notes in a row; the student says whether the 2nd note is higher or lower. Start far apart (open string 6 – open string 1), gradually closer (2 frets).
  2. Happy or sad: the teacher strums C or Am, G or Em (in random order); the student says "major / minor". Target: 8 out of 10 correct.
  3. Count the chord changes: the teacher plays 4 bars of 4 beats; the student raises a hand (or taps the table) whenever they hear the chord change, and at the end says "how many changes".
  4. Match the open string: the teacher plucks string 5 (A), the student sings "laaa" to match it; then string 4, string 3. No need to sing well — just match the pitch.
  5. Copy the rhythm: the teacher claps 1 bar (quarter notes, eighth notes), the student claps it back; next step: the student strums that rhythm on an Em chord.
  • At home: use the ▶ button on the strumming-pattern boxes and the virtual guitar 🎸 to listen and guess on your own.

Online teaching notes

  • For theory, screen-share the note-name table and the fretboard diagram; use the pointer on each fret.
  • Don't lecture theory for more than 10 minutes in a row — always have the student play straight away what they just learned (find C on string 5, play the C major scale).
  • Ear-training games: play each note clearly and loudly enough; Zoom sometimes swallows the first note — play one "primer" note before starting the question.
  • Weak connection: send a recording of the questions in advance (e.g. 10 major/minor questions), and the student answers via Zalo (Vietnam's main messaging app).

Common mistakes

  • Mixing up "upper string – lower string": string 1 (high E) is at the bottom when you hold the guitar but at the top in tab → always refer to strings by number.
  • Thinking "string 2, fret 1 is B sharp" — B and C are only a half step apart, so string 2, fret 1 is C.
  • Reading Am as "La trưởng" (A major) — a lower-case m means minor (thứ).
  • Memorising the note table without playing → forgotten straight away; every piece of theory must be tied to something done on the guitar.

Homework

  • Every day: play the C major scale in 1st position up – down 3 times, saying the note names as you play, tempo 60.
  • Find and play every C in 1st position (string 5, fret 3; string 2, fret 1).
  • Self-check: write out the 3 notes of C, G, Am, Em and D without looking at the table.
  • Listen to 2 favourite songs and guess which sections "sound sad" (lots of minor chords) and which "sound bright".

Completion criteria

  • Names the 7 notes correctly in both systems (Đô–C … Si–B) and the 6 open strings.
  • Plays the C major scale in 1st position up – down, naming the notes correctly.
  • Names the 3 notes of C and Am, and points out the 2 shared notes.
  • Gets at least 8 out of 10 major / minor questions right.
