Supplement — a bank of material for the teacher to assign when a student is moving fast, needs more pieces to practise the same skill, or wants to play "real songs" sooner. Everything here uses only the 5 Level 1 chords: Em, Am, C, G, D.

Objectives

  • Students have enough material to practise chord changes and strumming patterns without getting bored.
  • Students can play at least 1 melody and accompany that same melody (2 parts), then "play a duet with themselves".
  • Students know how to check for themselves whether a song suits Level 1.

How to use it

  • ★ = after Lesson 7 · ★★ = after Lesson 8 · ★★★ = after Lesson 10 / 11.
  • Each week, pick 1 progression + 1 piece — don't assign too much.
  • Start at tempo 60 and go up using the "tempo ladder" (Supplement 2).

A. Chord progressions by difficulty

Each chord gets 4 beats (1 bar of 4/4); repeat 4–8 times.

#ProgressionChange to watchLevel
1Em – AmEm → Am: move both fingers down 1 string, add finger 1★
2Am – Em – Am – EmAs above, faster: 2 beats per chord★
3C – AmAnchor fingers 1 and 2 stay put★★
4G – CFinger 3 moves from string 1 to string 5★★
5G – DOften "stalls" — place finger 3 (string 2) first★★
6G – C – D – GThe 3 major chords of the key of G★★
7Am – C – G – EmCombines the anchor finger (Am–C) and the guide finger (G–Em)★★
8G – Em – C – DA very common progression in pop / V-pop★★
9Em – C – G – DLike progression 8 but starting on a minor chord★★★
10C – G – Am – EmCommon in ballads★★★
  • Advanced variations (★★★): 2 beats per chord, or switch strumming pattern A → B → C (Lesson 10) every 4 rounds.
  • Classical track: play the same progressions but arpeggiate p-i-m-a (Supplement 1) instead of strumming.

D. "A duet with yourself" — a solution for online classes

Over the internet, teacher and student cannot play at the same time (see Lesson structure). Instead:

  1. The student records the accompaniment (e.g. Twinkle, pattern A, tempo 70) on their phone, with a count-in of "1 – 2 – 3 – 4".
  2. Play the recording through a speaker / headphones and play the melody along with it.
  3. Swap roles: record the melody, play the accompaniment along with it.
  4. Advanced: use a multitrack recording app (BandLab — free) to combine the 2 parts into one recording and send it to the teacher.
  • The teacher can also send their own accompaniment for the student to play the melody over — the student gets the feeling of "playing with the teacher".

E. Choosing Vietnamese songs for Level 1

Vietnamese students usually want to accompany songs they love as early as possible — this is a very strong motivator. Check a song against this list:

  • Uses only Em, Am, C, G, D (or can be brought down to these 5 chords with a capo — see below).

  • Slow to medium tempo (ballads, slow pop); avoid fast songs with several chord changes per bar.

  • Each chord lasts at least 2 beats.

  • Has a repeating chord progression (easy to memorise).

  • The student likes the song.

  • Using a capo to change key: a song in the key of A (chords A – D – E – F#m) → put the capo on fret 2 and play the shapes G – C – D – Em. On the virtual guitar 🎸, choose capo 2 to check.

  • Songs that need F, Bm or lots of 7th chords → save them for Level 2 (Lesson 9: F and Bm Barre Chords).

  • Suggested songs by level: Appendix 02.

  • Copyright: the curriculum lists only song titles and chords; do not copy lyrics or tabs of copyrighted songs into shared materials. Students look up the lyrics themselves on chord sites (e.g. hopamchuan, a popular Vietnamese chord site).

Online teaching notes

  • Single-note melodies (part C) are the easiest material to assess over Zoom — use them to check left-hand tone clarity.
  • Accompaniment pieces (part B): assess chord changes from the left-hand picture + recorded videos; strumming over Zoom is often compressed.
  • Send tabs and chord charts via Zalo (Vietnam's main messaging app) as sharp images, or send a link to this page.
  • When a student picks a Vietnamese song: ask them to send the song title + a link to the chord page before the lesson so the teacher can check it against the list in part E.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing a song that is too hard "because I love it" → giving up after a week. Solution: keep that song as a Level 2 goal and pick a similar, easier one.
  • Playing the melody from memory of the song, with wrong note values → always listen to the demo recording first.
  • Leaving out the count-in when recording the accompaniment → not knowing when to come in.

Homework

  • Each week: 1 progression (part A) + 1 piece (part B or C) at a suitable ★ level.
  • At least once during Level 1: record an accompaniment + play the melody over it (part D), and send the video to the teacher.
  • Send the teacher 1–2 Vietnamese songs you want to learn, with links to the chords.

Completion criteria

  • Plays at least 6 of the 10 progressions in part A, tempo 60, without stopping.
  • Plays at least 1 melody from part C and accompanies that piece from part B.
  • Completes one "duet with yourself" recording.
