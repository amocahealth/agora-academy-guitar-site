Level 1: Beginner · Lesson 17/17

Supplement 5: FAQ for beginners

CoreDraft

The questions beginners learning guitar online ask most: sore fingers, buzzing, choosing a guitar and strings, small hands, left-handedness, practising in an apartment, the humid nồm season, and how long until they can accompany songs.

Supplement — send new students a link to this page right after they sign up, before Lesson 1. Teachers use it to answer quickly on Zalo (Vietnam's main messaging app): "Please see question 4 on the FAQ page".

Objectives

  • Students can solve common problems on their own without waiting for the next lesson.
  • Teachers give consistent answers across classes.

Hands and body

1. My left-hand fingers really hurt — is that normal?

  • Yes. For the first 1–3 weeks the fingertips will be sore and red; after that they build calluses and stop hurting.
  • Practise short and often (2–3 × 10–15 minutes/day) instead of one long session.
  • Stop if you have: sharp pain in the wrist / elbow, numb fingers, blisters — see Supplement 1.

2. My hands are small / my fingers are short — can I still play?

  • Yes. Technique matters more than hand size: thumb low behind the neck, wrist pushed slightly forward.
  • Practise the finger exercises in 5th position (frets 5–8) first, where the frets are narrower, and only then move to 1st position.
  • Children: use a 1/2-size guitar (about ages 6–8) or a 3/4-size guitar (about ages 8–11); the teacher advises based on height.

3. What if I'm left-handed?

  • Many left-handed people learn on a standard guitar (the left hand frets — the "skilful" hand does the hard job).
  • If you want to play left-handed: you need a left-handed guitar (or have a shop reverse the strings, nut and saddle). Decide before Lesson 2 — switching later is very hard.
  • Tell the teacher so they keep the mirror image in mind when demonstrating.

4. Do I need to grow my right-hand nails?

  • Accompaniment track: no — use a pick or your fingertips.
  • Classical track: not yet at Level 1; from Level 2, grow the right-hand nails to a medium length (1–2 mm past the fingertip), filed smooth and round. Left-hand nails are always kept short.

Sound

5. The strings buzz — what do I do? Check in this order:

  1. Is the finger pressing close to the fret (the metal fret wire on the body side)?
  2. Are you using your fingertip, with the finger curved? If the finger pad touches the next string → that string is muted.
  3. Enough pressure? Try pressing a little harder.
  4. Open strings buzz too → the problem is the guitar (bowed neck, uneven frets) → have a guitar shop check it.

6. The guitar is really hard to press — is it the guitar?

  • Possibly. Cheap guitars often have high action (strings far from the frets). At the 12th fret, the gap between string 6 and the fret should be about 3 mm (acoustic) and 4 mm (classical).
  • A guitar shop can adjust the action (lowering the saddle, adjusting the truss rod) — low cost, big payoff for beginners.

7. My guitar keeps going out of tune?

  • New strings need 3–7 days to fully stretch — tuning several times a day is normal.
  • Temperature changes (air-conditioned room ↔ outdoors) knock the strings out of tune → tune every time before practising.
  • If it goes out of tune very quickly after the strings have settled: check the tuning pegs and have a shop look at it.

Guitars and accessories

8. Should I buy a classical or an acoustic guitar?

Classical (nylon strings)Acoustic (steel strings)
SuitsClassical track, children, people worried about sore fingersAccompaniment track, pop, ballads
FeelSoft strings, less painfulStiff strings, more painful for the first 1–2 weeks
NeckWiderNarrower, easier for barre chords
SoundWarm, round, quieterBright, loud, resonant
  • Not sure? Choose classical — switching to acoustic later is very easy; the other way round is harder.
  • Ask the teacher or someone who plays to go shopping with you; test it: tune to pitch, check the pitch at the 12th fret, sight down the neck for any bowing or twisting.

9. What do I need to buy?

  • Essential: guitar, gig bag, tuner app (or a clip-on tuner for the headstock), metronome app.
  • Good to have: phone holder, guitar stand, spare set of strings, capo (late Level 1), thin pick 0.46–0.6 mm (Accompaniment track), footstool (Classical track).

10. How often should I change strings?

  • Practising every day: every 2–3 months; dull tone, hard to keep in tune, rusty strings → change them.
  • Wash your hands before practice and wipe the strings afterwards → strings last much longer (especially in summer, when you sweat more).

11. Does the humid nồm season in the North / the rainy season in the South harm the guitar?

  • Yes. (Nồm is the damp, muggy spell in northern Vietnam around late winter and spring.) High humidity makes the wood swell, raises the action and rusts the strings quickly; too dry (long hours in an air-conditioned room) can crack the top.
  • Keep the guitar in its bag with desiccant packs (silica gel) inside during the nồm season; don't keep the guitar right by a window, in direct sunlight or directly under the air conditioner.
  • Ideal: room humidity of about 45–55%.

Practice

12. How long until I can accompany songs?

  • Following the curriculum: about 10 sessions (2.5–3 months at 1 session/week) — accompanying simple 3–4 chord songs, if you practise steadily for 20–30 minutes/day.
  • Songs with F, Bm, Bolero or Ballad rhythms: Level 2 (about 3 more months).

13. I live in an apartment — worried about noise for the neighbours when practising in the evening?

  • A classical guitar is quieter than an acoustic.
  • Pluck with your fingertips instead of a pick; strum lightly.
  • Tuck a thin cloth / string dampener near the bridge when practising chord changes.
  • Practise the left hand without strumming (silent chords, the 1-2-3-4 exercise) — still very effective.

14. Do I need to read music?

  • Level 1: no — we use tab and chord diagrams.
  • Classical track: starts reading treble-clef notation from Level 2. Accompaniment track: you only need to understand rhythm and chords.
  • Basic theory helps you learn faster: see Supplement 3.

15. Can I teach myself from YouTube?

  • Videos are great for watching examples, but nobody corrects your posture and mistakes. Bad habits (thumb squeezing the neck, bent wrist) are very hard to fix later.
  • The best combination: the course's lesson videos (watched in advance) + lessons with the teacher (fixing mistakes) + homework videos sent to the teacher (tracking progress).

16. What if I miss a few sessions / a few weeks?

  • Come back by reviewing the last lesson you had down solidly, rather than jumping straight into new material.
  • Your fingers may hurt again for a few days — that's normal.
  • Tell the teacher so they can adjust your path; Level 1 allows splitting a lesson over 2 sessions.

Learning online

17. My connection is patchy and the guitar sound drops out?

  • Sit close to the Wi-Fi router or use 4G mobile data; turn off other devices that are streaming video.
  • Turn on "Original sound for musicians" (Zoom) or turn off "Noise cancellation" (Google Meet) — the guitar sound being cut is usually caused by the filter, not the network.
  • If it's still poor: the teacher assesses you from pre-recorded videos, and the lesson focuses on what can be seen of your hands.

18. Why doesn't the teacher play along with me?

  • Audio over the internet is delayed by 150–500 ms — playing together would drift out of time and get messy. The teacher plays an example → stops → you play it back (call-and-response).
  • If you want to play along: use the accompaniment the teacher sends, or "play a duet with yourself" (Supplement 4).

Notes for teachers

  • Update this page when the same new question comes up from 3 or more students.
  • Answers about health (pain, numbness) always end with: stop practising and tell the teacher; never advise "practising through the pain".
  • For parents: send questions 2, 8, 9 and 12 separately before they buy a guitar for their child.

Completion criteria

  • The student has read this page before Lesson 1.
  • The student can check the 4 causes of buzzing on their own (question 5).
