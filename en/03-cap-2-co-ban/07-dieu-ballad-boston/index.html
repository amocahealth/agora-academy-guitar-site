Lesson 7: Ballad and Boston Styles

Accompaniment track⏱ 60 minDraft

The two most common 4/4 strumming styles in Vietnamese pop (nhạc trẻ) and Trịnh Công Sơn songs (nhạc Trịnh): Ballad and Boston, plus palm muting.

Objectives

  • Students accompany in the Ballad (slow 4/4 pop-ballad strum) and Boston (gently swaying 4/4 strum, as named in Vietnam) styles, counting aloud while strumming.
  • Perform a palm mute ("chát") with the edge of the right palm.
  • Accompany a full chord loop of a familiar song (open-position chords only).

Preparation

  • Teacher: demo video of each style (slow/medium), light drum backing track, list of suggested songs (title + chord progression).
  • Student: guitar, thin pick (optional), metronome.

Main content

  • Symbols: B = bass note (thumb or pick), ↓ = downstrum, ↑ = upstrum, (c) = palm mute, – = hand keeps moving but misses the strings.
  • Principle: the right hand swings steadily like a pendulum in eighth notes (down on the beat, up on the "and").
  • Ballad style (slow, 60–80 bpm) — in the counts below, và = "and":
    • Count: 1 – 2 – và – 3 – và – 4 – và
    • Strum: B – ↓ – ↑ – (–) – ↑ – ↓ – ↑
    • Muted variation: beats 2 and 4 become ↓(c) for a drum-like "chát".
Ballad style — one cell per eighth note 4/4

  3. 2
  4. &
  5. 3·
  6. &
  7. 4
  8. &

  1. 1B
  2. &·
  3. 2
  4. &
  5. 3·
  6. &
  7. 4

  • Boston style (medium, 80–100 bpm, light and "swaying"):
    • Count: 1 – 2 – và – 3 – 4 – và
    • Strum: B – ↓(c) – ↑ – B' – ↓(c) – ↑
    • Bass on beat 1 = the chord's root bass; beat 3 = alternate bass (neighbouring string).
Boston style — B = root bass, B' = alternate bass 4/4
Boston
  1. 1B
  2. &·
  3. 2
  4. &
  5. 3B'
  6. &·
  7. 4
  8. &
  • Note: each style can be played many ways; this is the basic version so everyone teaches it the same way.
  • Suggested songs — use title + chord progression only; students sing on their own:
    • Ballad: "Ước gì" (Võ Thiện Thanh), "Có chàng trai viết lên cây" (Phan Mạnh Quỳnh), "Nắng ấm xa dần" (Sơn Tùng M-TP).
    • Boston: "Tuổi đá buồn", "Còn tuổi nào cho em", "Biển nhớ" (Trịnh Công Sơn).
    • Practice loops with no barre chords: G – D – Em – C; Am – Dm – G – C.
  • Teachers should double-check each song's key and chords against a reliable source before assigning it.

Lesson flow (60 minutes)

  • 5' — Warm-up, tuning
  • 10' — Review 7th/sus chords; chord changes G – D – Em – C
  • 25' — "Pendulum" arm swing → Ballad (unmuted → muted) → Boston
  • 15' — Accompany the chord loop of one suggested song with the backing track
  • 5' — Summary, assign homework

Online teaching notes

  • Move the camera further back to show the right hand, guitar body and left hand — strumming needs the whole arm movement in view.
  • Strums and muted hits are easily cut by noise suppression → original sound is a must.
  • Never strum together with the student (100–300 ms latency throws off the timing): teacher plays 2 bars → mutes → student plays 2 bars.
  • Students count aloud while strumming — the teacher hears the count and can tell where they drift.
  • Play the backing track on the student's side (send the file beforehand), not through the teacher's audio share.
  • Pre-record demo videos with the B/↓/↑ symbols scrolling on screen.

Common mistakes

  • Right hand stopping at the "–" → losing the steady pulse.
  • Muting too long or too hard → flat, dead sound; the chord disappears.
  • Bass on the wrong string (especially Em/G on string 6 vs C/Am on string 5).
  • Late chord changes, a "hole" on beat 1.
  • Strumming too loud or too fast for the song.

Homework

  • Ballad and Boston on one chord (G) with a metronome, 5' per style.
  • Accompany the G – D – Em – C loop in both styles.
  • Pick 1 Ballad and 1 Boston song from the list and accompany its chord progression; film a 30-second clip.

Completion criteria

  • Plays the Ballad pattern correctly for 8 bars non-stop at 70 bpm
  • Plays the Boston pattern with palm mutes correctly at 90 bpm
  • Changes chords exactly on beat 1 without breaking the pulse
  • Accompanies a section of a song (singing aloud or in their head) in the right style
