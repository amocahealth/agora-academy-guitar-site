Lesson 8: Bolero and Slow Rock Styles

Accompaniment track⏱ 60 minDraft

Bolero (as played in Vietnamese nhạc vàng) and 12/8 Slow rock — two strumming styles everyone uses when singing with family and friends.

Objectives

  • Students accompany in the Bolero style (the slow, lilting Vietnamese bolero of nhạc vàng, "golden-era" sentimental songs — not the Cuban or Ravel bolero) with a two-note alternating bass.
  • Understand 12/8 time (4 beats, each divided into 3) and accompany in Slow rock (12/8 slow rock ballad feel).
  • Accompany the typical minor-key Bolero progression (Am – Dm – E7 – Am).

Preparation

  • Teacher: demo videos of both styles, Bolero (drums + bass) and Slow rock backing tracks, list of suggested songs.
  • Student: guitar, metronome with a triplet subdivision setting, chords Am, Dm, E7, C, G7.

Main content

  • Symbols as in lesson 7: B bass, ↓ down, ↑ up, (c) mute.
  • Bolero style (4/4, slow 60–80 bpm, "lilting") — và = "and":
    • Count: 1 – 2 – và – 3 – và – 4 – và
    • Strum: B – ↓ – ↑ – B' – ↑ – ↓ – ↑
    • B = main bass (Am: string 5), B' = alternate bass (Am: string 4 or string 6).
    • Light accent on beat 1, keep the hand relaxed; no palm muting.

    • Slow rock: "Tình nhỏ mau quên", "Chiếc lá cuối cùng", "Đêm nay ai đưa em về".
  • Teachers should check the style/key against the most common arrangement before assigning (some songs exist in several arrangements in different styles).

Lesson flow (60 minutes)

  • 5' — Warm-up, tuning
  • 10' — Review Ballad/Boston, watch the student's homework video
  • 25' — Bolero: alternating bass → add strums → Am – Dm – E7 – Am loop; Slow rock: count 12/8 → level 1 → level 2
  • 15' — Accompany a Bolero or Slow rock song of the student's choice with the backing track
  • 5' — Summary, assign homework

Online teaching notes

  • Camera further back with both hands in view; with a second camera, put it close on the right hand to check the B/B' basses.
  • Counting 12/8: teacher counts aloud + claps → student counts back (call-and-response), without the guitar yet.
  • Never play together; teacher plays one loop → student plays one loop.
  • Many older students love Bolero: let them choose the song to boost motivation, but keep the correct rhythm pattern.
  • Send the backing tracks and a 12/8 metronome video (triplets with accents) beforehand for home practice.
  • If the teacher wants to sing to demonstrate: sing only a short melody in the session and never put song lyrics into the materials.

Common mistakes

  • Playing Bolero as a Ballad (forgetting the alternate bass B' on beat 3).
  • Playing Bolero too fast and losing its "lilting" character.
  • Counting Slow rock as plain 4/4 (losing the triplet feel).
  • Slow E7 → Am change, missing the string-5 bass.

Homework

  • Bolero loop Am – Dm – E7 – Am, 60 → 72 bpm.
  • Slow rock level 1 → level 2 on the C – Am – Dm – G7 loop.
  • Accompany 1 Bolero and 1 Slow rock song from the list; send a video of one.

Completion criteria

  • Correct Bolero pattern with B and B' basses, 8 bars non-stop, 66 bpm
  • Slow rock level 2 with an even triplet feel
  • Tells Ballad / Bolero / Slow rock apart by ear when the teacher plays them
