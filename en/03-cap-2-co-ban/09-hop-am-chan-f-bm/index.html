Lesson 9: F and Bm Barre Chords

Core⏱ 60 minDraft

The first barre technique: from a half barre to E-shape F and Am-shape Bm, and how to practise without hurting your hand.

Objectives

  • Students understand the principle of the barre chord: finger 1 acts as the "nut".
  • Fret F (barre at fret 1, E shape) and Bm (barre at fret 2, Am shape) — strings ringing, not yet perfect.
  • Know the "easy" versions to use right away when accompanying while still working on the full barre.

Preparation

  • Teacher: close-up video of finger 1 (seen from above and below the neck), PDF diagram of the steps.
  • Student: a guitar with action (string height) that isn't too high — if it is hard to play, the teacher suggests taking it for a setup; a capo (to practise barring at higher frets first).

Main content

  • A 4-step path, no rushing:
    1. Half barre: start from Fmaj7 (xx3210 — not a barre yet: finger 1 frets only string 2 at fret 1, string 1 stays open), then flatten finger 1 across string 1 at fret 1 as well → small F (xx3211), the first real half barre.
    2. Full 6-string barre with nothing else fretted: check every string rings, at fret 5 first, then gradually move down to fret 1.
    3. Full F (133211): the E chord shape moved up to fret 1, with finger 1 barring.
    4. Bm (x24432): the Am chord shape moved up to fret 2; easy version: x x 4 4 3 2 (4 strings).
From Fmaj7 to F and Bm
  • Technique tips:
    • Roll finger 1 slightly onto its outer (bony) edge, not the soft fleshy pad.
    • Keep finger 1 right behind the fret (the metal bar), not in the middle of the fret space.
    • The pressure comes from the weight of the arm pulling back, not just from squeezing with the thumb.
    • Place the thumb around the middle of the back of the neck, opposite finger 2.
  • Short and often: 5 × 1 minute, with rests in between; stop immediately if the wrist hurts.
  • Application: C – G – Am – F; G – D – Em – Bm – C – G – C – D; Am – F – C – G.
  • Classical track: half barres (marked "½ C I", "C II") will appear in études.

Lesson flow (60 minutes)

  • 5' — Warm-up, stretch left-hand fingers and wrist
  • 10' — Review Bolero/Slow rock or arpeggio patterns (depending on track)
  • 25' — Steps 1 → 4, checking each string
  • 15' — Chord loops with F/Bm in Ballad style or with a p-i-m-a arpeggio pattern
  • 5' — Summary, assign homework; remind students to rest when tired

Online teaching notes

  • 2 camera angles needed: facing the neck + a close-up of finger 1 from above (phone on the music stand or looking down).
  • Ask the student to pick one string at a time, slowly → you can hear which strings are muted/buzzing.
  • Video compression can hide gaps under the finger → ask the student to describe what they feel, and review the high-resolution video they send.
  • Watch posture on camera: a raised left shoulder or sharply bent wrist means the force is coming from the wrong place.
  • Don't turn the lesson into "enduring pain": if the student (especially a child with small hands) can't manage yet, use the easy version and move on.
  • Send the demo video + a "5 minutes of barre practice a day" video with an on-screen timer.

Common mistakes

  • Strings 2 and 3 muted (they fall right under the creases of finger 1).
  • Squeezing too hard with the thumb → fatigue and pain.
  • Finger 1 too far from the fret.
  • Elbow clamped to the body, wrist bent.
  • Giving up after 2–3 days because "it doesn't ring" — it takes 2–4 weeks to get used to it.

Homework

  • 5 × 1 minute of barring every day (steps 2–4), resting in between.
  • C ↔ F changes (small F first, then full F), 2 minutes a day.
  • Loop G – D – Em – Bm – C – G – C – D, with the easy Bm.
  • Send a left-hand close-up video picking each string of F and Bm.

Completion criteria

  • Full F: at least 5/6 strings ring clearly
  • Bm: at least 4/5 strings ring clearly (or easy Bm 4/4 strings)
  • Changes C → F within 2 beats at 60 bpm
  • Knows when to use the easy versions while accompanying
