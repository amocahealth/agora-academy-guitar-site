Lesson 1: Positions II, V, VII and notes on the fretboard

Classical track⏱ 60 minDraft

The concept of position, note names up to fret 12, and playing melodies in positions II, V and VII.

Objectives

  • Students understand position (thế bấm): the position is named after the fret where finger 1 sits.
  • Students name the notes on all 6 strings correctly up to fret 12.
  • Students play the C major scale / short melodies in positions II, V and VII.
  • Students shift positions smoothly using a guide finger.

Preparation

  • Teacher: PDF fretboard chart (natural notes up to fret 12), position-shift exercise, second camera filming the neck.
  • Student: tuned guitar, metronome, pencil, printed fretboard chart.

Main content

  • Position = where finger 1 sits; each finger covers 1 fret (the "one finger per fret" rule).
  • Quick landmarks: fret 5 (string 6 = A, string 5 = D), fret 7 (string 6 = B, string 5 = E), fret 12 = the open strings again.
  • The same pitch appears in several places (e.g. open string 1 E = string 2 fret 5 = string 3 fret 9).
  • Position II: D major / A major scale, 1 octave.
  • Position V: A minor and C major scales — the most common position in classical repertoire.
  • Position VII: E minor / E major scale — preparation for Romance (lesson 11).
  • Shifting: the guide finger glides lightly along the string, the hand is not lifted, and the thumb behind the neck moves with it.
  • Position markings in classical scores: Roman numerals (II, V, VII) and "C" / "½C" for barres.
  • (Accompaniment track, shortened) only note names on strings 6 and 5 are needed → used straight away for barre chords in lesson 2.

Lesson flow (60 minutes)

  • 5' — Warm-up / greetings, check audio and the second camera.
  • 10' — Review: the Level 2 test, the étude currently being practised.
  • 25' — New material: the concept of position, the fret 5/7/12 landmarks, the "find the note" game.
  • 15' — Guided practice: scales in position II → V → VII, an 8-bar position-shift exercise.
  • 5' — Summary, assign homework.

Online teaching notes

  • Play "find the note" as call-and-response: the teacher names a note + string, the student answers by playing it; the teacher waits out the latency before saying right/wrong.
  • Never play at the same time as the student (150–300 ms latency throws the timing off) → the teacher plays once, the student repeats.
  • The student's second camera films along the neck from fret 1 to fret 12 so you can see finger 1 is on the right fret.
  • Screen-share the PDF fretboard chart and use Zoom's "Annotate" tool to circle the fret 5 and 7 landmarks.
  • Ask students to record the position-shift exercise themselves after the lesson (a recording is clearer than audio over the network).

Common mistakes

  • Looking at the fret-marker dots instead of counting frets → confusing position V with position VII.
  • Lifting the whole hand when shifting → notes cut off, phrases broken.
  • Thumb behind the neck not following → finger 4 can't reach.
  • Getting sharp/flat names wrong between two natural notes (E–F and B–C have no note in between).

Homework

  • 5' every day: name random notes on strings 6 and 5 (using an app or flashcards).
  • C major scale in position V and position VII, metronome 60 → 80.
  • The I → V → VII position-shift exercise in the PDF; record one video at the end of the week.

Completion criteria

  • Names 10/10 random notes on strings 6 and 5 correctly within 30 seconds.
  • Plays scales in positions II, V and VII without looking at the chart.
  • Shifts positions without breaking the sound at tempo 60.
