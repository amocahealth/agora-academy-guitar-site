Lesson 11: Romance Anónimo — the complete piece

Classical track⏱ 60 minDraft

Romance Anónimo (Romance de Amor) complete, with both the E minor and E major sections: triplet arpeggios, a-finger melody, barres and finger stretches.

Objectives

  • Students play the complete Romance Anónimo (also known as Romance de Amor): the E minor section and the E major section.
  • The melody (finger a, string 1) stands out clearly above an even i-m arpeggio.
  • Students get through the barres and finger stretches in high positions without breaking the flow of the arpeggio.

Preparation

  • Teacher: PDF score (anonymous work, public domain) with fingerings and positions; separate videos of the right hand and the left hand.
  • Student: printed score; the first section already playable (if learned in Level 2) — this session completes the whole piece.

Main content

  • 3/4 time, one triplet per beat: a – m – i; the thumb (p) holds the bass at the start of the beat.
  • Section 1 (E minor): position I → position VII (barre) → higher up; the passage reaching fret 12 needs a finger-4 stretch.
  • Section 2 (E major): many consecutive barres in higher positions; a brighter colour, rounder tone.
  • Review lesson 1 (positions), lesson 2 (barre chords), lesson 4 (E minor – E major keys).
  • Right hand: a plays louder (apoyando is possible), m-i softly; practise "a loud – m i soft" on open strings.
  • Left hand: prepare the barre a beat early; release it at the right moment and keep the guide finger when shifting.
  • Suggested dynamics: section 1 mp, dolce; section 2 brighter, crescendo to the peak of the phrase; end diminuendo, possibly with a fermata.
  • "Rote-learning" problem: students often learn it from social-media videos → re-check fingering and rhythm against the score.

Lesson flow (60 minutes)

  • 5' — Warm-up / greetings, check audio and the right-hand camera.
  • 10' — Review: the Tárrega piece, video feedback.
  • 25' — New material: finish section 1 (the position VII barre passage, the stretch); start section 2.
  • 15' — Guided practice: the difficult spots in section 2, the transition from section 1 → section 2.
  • 5' — Summary, assign homework (2 weeks for section 2).

Online teaching notes

  • Two cameras: the main camera shows the whole body, the second camera (phone) films the right hand to check that a – m – i aren't getting mixed up.
  • Screen-share the PDF and mark the barres (CVII, ½C…) and the bar numbers of the difficult spots.
  • Call-and-response in 2-bar chunks in the barre passages; the student plays slowly while the teacher only listens and does not play along.
  • Melody / arpeggio balance is hard to hear over Zoom → turn on "Original sound for musicians"; base final judgements on videos the student records.
  • Remind students to rest their hand after long barre passages; ask directly about fatigue, since it's hard to see on camera.

Common mistakes

  • Arpeggio as loud as the melody → it sounds like a finger exercise, not a song.
  • Breaking the triplet flow when changing barres.
  • Strings 2–3 not sounding in the barres of the E major section.
  • Speeding up in the easy passages and slowing down in the hard ones.

Homework

  • Week 1: section 1 complete with a metronome, at a steady target tempo.
  • Week 2: section 2 phrase by phrase; join section 1 → section 2.
  • 5' every day: left hand only on the barre positions (no plucking) to practise shifting.
  • Send a video of the complete piece at the end of week 2 — used as a draft for lesson 12.

Completion criteria

  • Plays both sections through without stopping.
  • The a-finger melody stands out clearly; even triplets.
  • Barres ring clearly, shifts without breaks.
