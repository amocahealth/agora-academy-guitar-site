Lesson 1: Tremolo technique

Classical track⏱ 60 minDraft

p-a-m-i tremolo: finger mechanics, slow even practice, rhythmic exercises and applying it to repertoire.

Objectives

  • Students understand how tremolo (p-a-m-i) works: the thumb plays the bass while a-m-i repeat a single melody note.
  • Play tremolo on open strings evenly at 60 bpm (one p-a-m-i group per beat).
  • Know at least 3 evenness exercises (dotted rhythms, shifting accents, gradual speed-up).
  • Apply it to a short passage with a moving bass.

Preparation

  • Teacher: a second camera with a close-up of the right hand (from above or from floor level); a metronome shared directly through the call audio.
  • Teacher: a tremolo study the teacher has written over Am – Dm – E – Am (no copyrighted material).
  • Student: right-hand nails filed evenly (a-m-i the same length, smooth edges); metronome app; phone stand to film the right hand.

Main content

  • Structure: p (bass) → a → m → i on the same melody string; 4 equal notes, not "1 + 3".
  • Relaxed wrist; movement comes from the middle finger joints, not "scratching" from the wrist.
  • a-m-i play tirando (free stroke); p may play apoyando (rest stroke) to bring out the bass.
  • Nail contact point: the same spot on the nail for a, m and i → even tone.
  • Practice order:
    • Step 1: a-m-i only, on open string 1, no p.
    • Step 2: add p, alternating on strings 4, 5 and 6.
  • Step 3: p changes strings following the chord progression; melody stays on string 1, then string 2.
  • Rhythmic variants:
    • Long–short and short–long dotted rhythms.
    • Accent each finger in turn: p, a, m, i.
    • "Stop" groups (p-a-m-i-p, hold for 1 beat) for control.
  • Speeding up: add 4 bpm only after 3 clean run-throughs in a row.
  • Link to repertoire: tremolo is the core technique of Recuerdos de la Alhambra (Tárrega) — the opening section is studied in Lesson 5.

Lesson flow (60 minutes)

  • 5' — Warm-up / greetings, sound check (turn on original sound)
  • 10' — Review p-i-m-a arpeggios, check nails on the close-up camera
  • 25' — Introduce tremolo, steps 1 → 3, dotted-rhythm exercise
  • 15' — Guided practice: the Am – Dm – E – Am study at 50–60 bpm
  • 5' — Summary, assign homework

Online teaching notes

  • Zoom/Meet audio compression makes tremolo notes "blur" together → turn on "Original sound" at both ends and turn off noise suppression.
  • Latency makes playing together impossible → the teacher plays, the student plays back; never play at the same time.
  • Ask the student to film the right hand with a second phone (angled from below) to see whether the a finger is "lazy".
  • Judge evenness from the recording the student sends after the lesson (uncompressed audio), not just from the call.
  • Use the metronome on the student's device; don't play a metronome through the call (it lags and drifts).

Common mistakes

  • Weak a finger → the first note of the group is quieter; fix with the accent-on-a exercise.
  • "Galloping" rhythm: p-a long, m-i short; fix by counting "1-2-3-4" evenly out loud.
  • Hand bouncing with each note → rest p lightly or keep the wrist still.
  • Uneven nail lengths → uneven volume; check nails before practising.
  • Speeding up before the slow tempo is even.

Homework

  • 10' daily: a-m-i on open strings 1 and 2, 60 bpm, both dotted rhythms.
  • 10' daily: the Am – Dm – E – Am study with the bass changing strings.
  • Log the highest clean tempo each day in the practice plan (Appendix 03).
  • Send one 60-second close-up video of the right hand at the end of the week.

Completion criteria

  • Open-string tremolo even at 72 bpm (4-note groups per beat) for 30 seconds.
  • The 4 notes in each group are close to equal in volume (check on a recording).
  • Plays the study with the moving bass without breaking the beat.
  • Wrist relaxed, not bouncing with the notes.
