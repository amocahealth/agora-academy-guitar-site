Lesson 6: Extended chords and reharmonization

Accompaniment track⏱ 60 minDraft

9, 11 and 13 chords; how to drop notes on guitar; reharmonizing the chord progression of a Vietnamese song.

Objectives

  • Students understand how extended chords (9, 11, 13) are built from 7th chords.
  • Play at least 8 common extended-chord voicings.
  • Know which notes to drop (the 5th first, sometimes the root) to fit 4–5 strings.
  • Reharmonize a simple progression using 4 techniques: adding colour, inserting ii–V, secondary dominants, tritone substitution.

Preparation

  • Teacher: the voicing chart (below) as a PDF; a Vietnamese song the student accompanied in Level 3 (title and chord progression only).
  • Student: guitar in tune; paper for writing progressions; a backing-track app or looper if available.

Main content

  • Review: 7th chord = 1-3-5-7; extend with 9 (= 2), 11 (= 4), 13 (= 6) in the upper octave.
  • Dropping notes on guitar: keep the 3rd and 7th (they define the quality) and the colour notes (9/11/13); drop the 5th first, and the root if a bass player is covering it.
  • Common voicings (string 6 → 1):
    • Cadd9 x32033 · Cmaj9 x32430 · C9 x32333
    • Am9 x05557 · Am11 x05533 · Em9 022032
    • Dm9 x53555 · G9 320201 · G13 3x3455
    • Fmaj7#11 1x2200 · E7#9 07678x
Common extended chords (+ Db9 for the tritone substitution)
  • Sound colours:
    • maj9: dreamy, suits ballads and bossa nova.
    • m9, m11: warm, "jazzy", suits nhạc Trịnh (songs of Trịnh Công Sơn) and modern love songs.
    • 13, 7#9: tense, used on the dominant chord before resolving to the tonic.
  • Reharmonization — example progression C – Am – F – G:
    • Add colour: Cmaj9 – Am9 – Fmaj7#11 – G13.
    • Insert ii–V: … – Dm9 – G13 – Cmaj9.
    • Secondary dominants: insert A7 before Dm, E7 before Am.
    • Tritone substitution: Db9 in place of G7 (bass moves down a semitone to C).
  • Principle: the song's melody must fit (or at least not clash with) the new chord — always sing it through.

Lesson flow (60 minutes)

  • 5' — Warm-up / greetings, sound check
  • 10' — Review 7th chords and movable barre shapes
  • 25' — Building 9/11/13 chords, voicing chart, 4 reharmonization techniques
  • 15' — Guided practice: reharmonize the progression of the chosen Vietnamese song
  • 5' — Summary, assign homework

Online teaching notes

  • Chords with many colour notes sound "muddy" over the network → have the student arpeggiate string by string so the teacher can hear each note.
  • Screen-share the voicing chart; use the pointer to show strings/frets while explaining.
  • Before–after comparison: the teacher accompanies the same phrase twice (original and reharmonized) so the student hears the difference.
  • The student records their reharmonized progression and sends it before the next lesson; give written feedback with timestamps.

Common mistakes

  • Using colour chords everywhere → the song loses its character.
  • Colour notes clashing with the melody (e.g. maj7 when the melody holds the root).
  • Hard voicings but slow changes → broken rhythm; choose voicings close together on the neck.
  • Forgetting that a singer needs to hear the root/bass clearly when you accompany alone.

Homework

  • Memorise the 11 voicings in the chart; move between them in ii–V–I in 3 keys (C, G, F).
  • Reharmonize a whole Vietnamese song you have learned; write out 2 versions (light and rich).
  • Record your favourite version while singing (or humming the melody).

Completion criteria

  • Plays 8/11 extended voicings cleanly.
  • Can explain which notes make up 9, 11 and 13 chords.
  • Reharmonizes a song using at least 2 different techniques.
  • The melody does not clash with the new chords (teacher checks the recording).
