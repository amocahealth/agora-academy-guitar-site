Lesson 8: Accompanying a singer

Accompaniment track⏱ 60 minDraft

Intros, endings, transposing to suit the singer, following a singer who rushes, drags or forgets the words; communicating on stage.

Objectives

  • Students write their own intro and outro for a song.
  • Quickly find a key that suits the singer and transpose on the spot, with or without a capo.
  • Follow the singer: wait, cut short or repeat a section when the singer comes in early/late or forgets the words.
  • Know the basic signals between accompanist and singer.

Preparation

  • Teacher: a well-known Vietnamese song (title + chord progression), e.g. a lyrical ballad or a nhạc Trịnh song (by Trịnh Công Sơn) the student already knows.
  • Teacher: arrange a "singer" (another student, a family member of the student, or the teacher singing) for the practice part.
  • Student: capo; the song's chord chart written in scale degrees (I, IV, V…) instead of chord names.

Main content

  • Intro — 3 formulas:
    • Use the last 4 bars of the chorus.
    • I – vi – IV – V (or ii – V – I) twice, ending on V to "invite" the singer in.
    • A sustained chord + harmonics (Lesson 3) for slow songs.
  • Outro:
    • Repeat the last line 2–3 times, slowing down (ritardando).
    • End on an extended chord (I maj9 / I add9) or with rasgueado (Lesson 2) for upbeat songs.
  • Finding the singer's key:
    • Ask for the song's lowest and highest notes; have the singer try the highest phrase.
    • For the same song, men usually sing about a perfect 4th to a 5th (≈ 2.5–3.5 whole tones) lower than women.
  • Transposing:
    • Think in degrees (I, IV, V, vi) → changing key just means changing the root.
    • Capo: keep familiar shapes, clamp a fret to raise the key.
    • Key change mid-song (modulation) up ½ or 1 tone in the last chorus — link it with the V chord of the new key.
  • Following the singer:
    • Listen to their breathing and the words; don't watch your own hands.
    • Singer comes in early → drop the rest of the bar and jump with them; late → repeat the current chord.
    • Singer forgets the words → keep the progression going, sing the first word of the line as a prompt, or play the melody.
  • Stage signals (band experience): nod = come in; raised hand = stop/end; circling finger = repeat; make eye contact before every transition.

Lesson flow (60 minutes)

  • 5' — Warm-up / greetings, sound check
  • 10' — Review extended chords, listen to last week's reharmonization
  • 20' — Intro/outro, finding the key, transposing, stage signals
  • 20' — Guided practice: accompany the "singer", with surprises (early entry, forgotten words, key change)
  • 5' — Summary, assign homework

Online teaching notes

  • Singing and accompanying together over the network is impossible because of latency → the singer and student must be in the same room (family, friend), or use a vocal recording (a self-recorded a cappella track) played on the student's device.
  • Alternative: the teacher sends in advance a vocal track (sung by the teacher) with deliberate "situations" (early entries, stops).
  • The teacher observes and assesses via a wide-angle camera showing both accompanist and singer.
  • Check the balance: on the teacher's end the voice must be clearer than the guitar.
  • Low-latency software (JamKazam, SonoBus) is an option if both sides have a good connection — test it before the lesson.

Common mistakes

  • Guitar louder than the voice; dense strumming in the verse.
  • Stopping when the singer makes a mistake → the whole song falls apart; always keep time and carry on.
  • Intro too long, or not ending on the chord that "invites" the singer in.
  • Choosing the key by the accompanist's habit rather than the singer's range.
  • Staring at the fretboard the whole song, no eye contact.

Homework

  • Write 2 different intros and 2 different outros for the chosen song.
  • Play the song's progression in 3 different keys without a capo.
  • Accompany a family member singing; film it wide-angle and send it to the teacher.

Completion criteria

  • The intro leads into the vocals at the right place and the singer comes in naturally.
  • Transposes a song to another key within 1 minute.
  • Handles at least 2 surprise situations without stopping the music.
  • Sensible guitar volume; the voice always stands out.
