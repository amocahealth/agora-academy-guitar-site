Level 4: Advanced · Lesson 9/12

Lesson 9: Basic improvisation

Core⏱ 60 minDraft

Improvising on the minor/major pentatonic and harmonic minor scales; building call-and-response phrases over a backing track.

Objectives

  • Students play the A minor pentatonic scale from memory in 2 positions (position V and position VIII).
  • Know how to use the same shapes as the C major pentatonic.
  • Play the A harmonic minor scale and know how to use G# over an E7 chord.
  • Improvise 8–16 bars in call-and-response form over a backing track.

Preparation

  • Teacher: 2 self-recorded backing tracks: (1) Am – Dm – Am – E7 (slow rock / slow bolero); (2) Am – G – F – E (rumba).
  • Teacher: pentatonic and harmonic minor scale diagrams as images.
  • Student: can play the backing track on their own device (external speaker or one-ear headphones); looper app (optional).

Main content

  • A minor pentatonic: A – C – D – E – G (no 2nd or 6th degree → hard to play a "wrong" note).
    • Shape 1 (fret 5) and shape 2 (fret 8).
    • The same notes = C major pentatonic (C – D – E – G – A); only the "home" note changes.
  • A harmonic minor: A – B – C – D – E – F – G#.
    • G# gives a Spanish / "Eastern" colour; target G# when the backing is on E7.
    • Evokes the sound of bolero and classical Spanish music.
  • Building phrases:
    • Start with 3 notes and simple rhythms; few notes, but purposeful.
    • Call and response: phrase 1 (2 bars) ends unresolved, phrase 2 (2 bars) resolves to the root.
    • Repeat and vary a motif: keep the rhythm, change the notes; keep the notes, change the rhythm.
    • End phrases on a chord tone of the chord that is sounding.
  • Ornaments: legato (slurs — Level 3), slide, vibrato; the Classical track uses classical vibrato.
  • Stage experience: "silence is a note too" — a short solo with a clear idea beats lots of fast notes.

Lesson flow (60 minutes)

  • 5' — Warm-up / greetings, sound check
  • 10' — Review: watch the accompanying-a-singer video, give feedback
  • 25' — Pentatonic in 2 positions, harmonic minor, call-and-response rules
  • 15' — Guided practice: improvising over the backing track, teacher "calls", student "responds"
  • 5' — Summary, assign homework

Online teaching notes

  • The backing track must play on the student's device, not through the call (latency throws the timing off in both directions).
  • Call and response works well online because nobody has to play at the same time: the teacher plays 2 bars, stops, the student answers with 2 bars.
  • The student wears a headphone in one ear for the backing track and leaves the other ear free for the guitar → stops the backing track leaking into the mic and drowning the guitar.
  • Ask the student to record themselves (backing track + guitar) with a phone app and send the file so the timing can be judged accurately.
  • Students afraid of playing "wrong" on camera → the teacher improvises with deliberate mistakes first to take the pressure off.

Common mistakes

  • Running the scale up and down instead of making phrases.
  • Playing non-stop with no rests.
  • Ending phrases on notes outside the chord, without resolution.
  • Using G# over Am/Dm → sounds harsh.
  • Losing the backing track's beat while concentrating on the fingers.

Homework

  • A minor pentatonic in 2 positions, up and down with the metronome at 80 bpm.
  • Every day 5 minutes improvising over backing track 1, 5 minutes over backing track 2.
  • Write out 1 "call" phrase and 3 different "response" phrases.
  • Send a recording of your best 16 bars of improvisation this week.

Completion criteria

  • Plays A minor pentatonic in 2 positions and A harmonic minor from memory.
  • Improvises 8 bars without losing the backing track's beat.
  • At least 1 clear call-and-response pair.
  • Uses G# in the right place, over the E7 chord.
