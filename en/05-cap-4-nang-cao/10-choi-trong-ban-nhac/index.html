Lesson 10: Playing in a band

Core⏱ 60 minDraft

The guitar's roles in a band (rhythm, lead, second guitar), sharing the frequency range, stage experience and rehearsal rules.

Objectives

  • Students understand the roles of rhythm guitar, lead guitar and second guitar in a band.
  • Know how to "make room" for bass, keyboard and vocals: choice of voicing, register and note density.
  • Know rehearsal etiquette and how to prepare for a real gig.
  • Read a chord chart with structure, section markers and ending cues.

Preparation

  • Teacher: prepare 2–3 real stories from your own band experience (incidents and how they were handled).
  • Teacher: a multitrack recording made by the teacher: drums, bass, keyboard — no guitar.
  • Student: headphones; the exercise chord chart (sent by the teacher in advance); an electric guitar too, if they have one.

Main content

  • Guitar roles:
    • Rhythm: locks in with the drums, harmonises with the keyboard.
    • Lead: intro lines, solos, fills between vocal lines.
    • Second guitar: high positions, short muted chords, arpeggios so as not to double guitar 1.
  • Sharing the sonic "space":
    • The bass already has the root → the guitar drops strings 5–6 and uses 3–4-string chords in high positions.
    • Keyboard playing thick chords → the guitar plays rhythmic figures with palm mute.
    • Don't play over the singer's key lines; put fills in the gaps.
  • Reading chord charts: Intro / Verse / Chorus / Bridge / Outro; repeat signs, "x2", "Coda", "Fine".
  • Rehearsal rules:
    • Arrive early, tune up and check gear before start time.
    • Don't noodle while the band leader is talking.
    • Know your part before the group rehearsal.
    • Write changes on the chord chart straight away; confirm tempo, key and structure.
    • Give feedback on the music, not the person.
  • Stage experience:
    • Soundcheck: listen to the monitor and make clear requests ("more vocals, less bass").
    • Carry spare strings, capo, batteries, cables and a clip-on tuner.
    • If you make a mistake, keep going — the audience rarely notices if the band doesn't stop.
    • Watch the drummer and singer to catch transitions and endings.

Lesson flow (60 minutes)

  • 5' — Warm-up / greetings, sound check
  • 10' — Review improvisation, listen to the 16-bar recording
  • 25' — Guitar roles, sharing sonic space, rehearsal rules, stage stories
  • 15' — Guided practice: student plays along with the multitrack, first as rhythm, then as lead
  • 5' — Summary, assign homework

Online teaching notes

  • The multitrack plays on the student's device; the teacher hears the overall result through the call or from a recorded file.
  • Simulate a "band rehearsal": the teacher plays band leader and cues section changes with hand signals on camera — practice in watching for cues.
  • Online group classes: students take turns recording their part over the same backing track and the teacher combines them into a virtual band.
  • Keep the stage stories short and tied to concrete situations; avoid long monologues while students just sit and listen.

Common mistakes

  • Playing all 6 strings all the time → swamps the bass and keyboard.
  • Not listening to the band, only to yourself.
  • Not knowing the song's structure → coming in at the wrong section.
  • Turning up gradually during rehearsal (volume war).
  • Stopping to fix mistakes in the middle of a gig.

Homework

  • Play the multitrack song in 3 versions: full rhythm, 3-string high-position rhythm, lead/fills.
  • Write a complete chord chart for a favourite Vietnamese song (chords and structure only).
  • List your own "gig bag".
  • (Optional) Join a group rehearsal with friends or a school band, and note 3 things you learned.

Completion criteria

  • Plays along with the multitrack without drifting off the beat or covering the bass.
  • Switches between rhythm and lead roles on cue.
  • Reads and follows a chord chart with repeats/Coda correctly.
  • Can name 5 rehearsal rules.
