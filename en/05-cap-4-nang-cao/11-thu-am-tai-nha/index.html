Lesson 11: Recording and filming performances at home

Core⏱ 60 minDraft

Minimum gear, mic placement for acoustic/classical guitar, filming, basic post-production and preparing a performance video.

Objectives

  • Students choose a setup that fits their budget (from phone-only to audio interface + microphone).
  • Place the mic correctly for classical/acoustic guitar, avoiding boominess and room noise.
  • Film a performance video that is well lit, with both hands clearly in frame.
  • Basic post-production: trim start and end, balance levels, sync good audio to the video.

Preparation

  • Teacher: demonstrate your own recording setup (shown via a second camera); 2 sample recordings of the same passage (mic placed correctly / incorrectly).
  • Student: phone + stand; (if available) USB audio interface, condenser mic; closed-back headphones; free software: Audacity, Reaper (trial) or BandLab; CapCut / DaVinci Resolve for video editing.

Main content

  • Three gear levels:
    • Basic: phone + a high-quality recording app, with automatic processing turned off.
    • Intermediate: USB mic or a phone-mounted mic.
    • Advanced: USB audio interface + 1–2 condenser mics.
  • Mic placement for guitar:
    • Aim at the neck–body joint (frets 12–14), 20–30 cm away.
    • Don't aim straight at the sound hole (boomy, too much bass).
    • Two mics (stereo): one at the 12th fret, one aimed at the top near the bridge.
  • Home studio: a small room full of soft furnishings (curtains, blankets, wardrobe) beats an empty room; switch off fans/air conditioning while recording.
  • Recording level (gain): peaks around –12 to –6 dB, never hitting the red.
  • Filming:
    • Phone in landscape, framing the whole guitar and the face.
    • Main light from the front/side; avoid a window behind you.
    • Lock exposure and focus; shoot 1080p at 30 or 60 fps.
  • Post-production: trim start/end, normalise volume, add a little light reverb, sync separately recorded audio to the video using a hand clap at the start.
  • Red light syndrome: record many takes and pick the best; treat each take like a real performance.

Lesson flow (60 minutes)

  • 5' — Warm-up / greetings, sound check
  • 10' — Review playing with a band; look at the chord chart the student wrote
  • 25' — Gear, mic placement, the recording room, filming, post-production
  • 15' — Guided practice: student sets up on the spot, records a 30-second test and sends the file during the lesson
  • 5' — Summary, assign homework

Online teaching notes

  • This is the most "naturally online" lesson: the teacher guides the student through adjusting the setup in their own room via the camera.
  • Ask the student to pan the camera so the teacher can see the whole room (seating position, windows, light sources).
  • Receive recordings via Google Drive/Zalo (a Vietnamese messaging app) as original files (Zalo/Messenger compress video — send as a file attachment, not as a video).
  • The teacher listens to files on good headphones; never judge recording quality through the call audio.
  • Send the recording checklist in advance so the student can go through it when recording at home.

Common mistakes

  • Mic too close to the sound hole → boomy.
  • Recording level too high → clipping; too low → lots of hiss when the volume is raised.
  • The phone app turning on noise reduction / automatic gain by itself.
  • Backlighting: face and hands in shadow.
  • Filming in portrait, cutting off the end of the neck.

Homework

  • Record and film a complete piece/song you have learned (intended for the recital).
  • Record 2 versions with 2 different mic positions, compare them and write notes.
  • Edit a finished video (trim, balance levels, title with song name – composer).
  • Send it to the teacher at least 3 days before session 12.

Completion criteria

  • Recording free of clipping and boominess, with low background noise.
  • Video in landscape, well lit, both hands clearly visible.
  • Audio and picture in sync.
  • Song title and composer correctly shown in the video.
